## Core Themes

### Narrative Tone
- **Slice of life with depth**: Balances everyday moments with serious, impactful events
- **Real stakes**: Characters can and will die. Betrayals happen. There will also be great times for the characters.
- **Found family**: The player builds bonds that may become more important than returning home

### Tonal Reference
- **The Wandering Inn (Pirateaba)**: The closest published touchstone for the tone we want. Cozy daily texture (food, walking, work, conversation) coexisting with sudden real violence and real death. Earnest, not ironic. Deep character interiority. RPG-style mechanics treated as natural facts of the world. Long, patient pacing where a quiet chapter has equal weight to a battle chapter. Walking and travel as meaningful, not just connective tissue — which maps directly onto StepQuest's step-based core loop.

### What Drives Human Conflict
- The corruption does NOT affect human minds
- Fear, hunger, and desperation drive people to conflict
- Human nature under pressure - not magical mind control

### The Player's Motivation
- Primary: Wants to return to their own world, their home
- Secondary: As they bond with characters, that desire may fade or decrease
- Tertiary: Gets invested in the world's problems and people

## Magic System

### The Nature of Magic

**Origin**: Magic is a natural energy produced by the planet itself. It regenerates over time - even after being completely drained, the planet will slowly produce more over centuries and millennia.

**Ambient Magic**: Magic exists throughout the environment - atmosphere, ground, water. It permeates everything.

### How Magic Interacts with Life

**Passive Absorption**: All living beings naturally absorb ambient magic without conscious effort - humans, animals, plants, all life forms.

**Enhancement, Not Sustenance**: Magic does NOT sustain life. Creatures can survive without magic entirely. However, magic ENHANCES quality of life:
- Increased vitality and energy
- Sharper mental clarity
- Better physical health
- Improved crop yields
- General sense of well-being

**Clarification - Highly Magical Life**: The rule above describes ordinary surface life. Some highly magic-dependent subterranean creatures are a special case: lack of magic does not kill them outright, but can force them into deep slumber or biological suspension until ambient magic rises again.

**Symbiosis**: The relationship between the planet and its inhabitants is symbiotic. The planet produces magic; life benefits from it.

### Magic Users

- Magic exists and is accepted in society
- Not everyone can actively perform magic - this ability is rare
- The reasons why some can use magic and others cannot are not fully understood
- Magic is more common now than centuries ago, as ambient levels have been rising

### Otherworlders and Magic

**Natural Affinity**: People from non-magical worlds have a natural affinity for magic. Having never been exposed to it, they never built up tolerance. This makes perceiving and learning to manipulate magic easier for them. This is distinct from natives born during the magic-poor centuries: those generations had no meaningful ambient magic to learn from or practice with, and later native generations grew up alongside slowly rising magic levels, building ordinary tolerance as they developed. Otherworlders arrive with bodies and senses formed outside the planet's magic cycle, then encounter magic all at once. However, this affinity only affects active magic use — otherworlders absorb ambient magic passively at the same rate as anyone else.

**Not an Intended Gift**: This affinity is a natural consequence of their origin, not something the ancients deliberately gave them. While the ancients noted otherworlders' natural talent during the brief summoning period before the seal, this affinity became irrelevant once the seal took effect - there was no magic left to sense or use. Only now, with magic returning, would this trait actually matter.

**Not Heritable**: This affinity applies only to first-generation otherworlders - those actually summoned from non-magical worlds. Their children, born in a world with magic, develop normal tolerance like anyone else, even if ambient levels are low at the time of birth. Ancient records note that summoned people showed natural talent with magic, but their descendants displayed no such gift.

### Corrupted Magic

**Effects on Living Beings** (who passively absorb it):
- Makes humans sick (physical illness)
- Makes animals sick, which causes them to become violent and aggressive (pain response, not mind control)
- Causes crops to fail

**Visibility**: Corrupted magic affects ambient mana, not the physical landscape. The terrain itself is not visibly damaged or discolored. The effects are biological - sickness in living beings, crop failure - not environmental destruction. There is no visible atmospheric pollution or magical fog.

**The Dead-Zone Exception**: While corruption itself is invisible, sustained exposure at very high concentration kills life outright — not just makes it sick. A region soaked in dense corruption for long enough loses its plants, its animals, and eventually any trace of living growth. The result *looks* dead: bare soil, dried-out tree skeletons, no birdsong, no insects, no game. The land is not magically tainted in itself, and the soil can support life again once corruption levels drop — but during the exposure, and for a long time after, it appears scorched. The historical extreme is the wide zone around the Original's lair in ancient times: high-concentration corruption was sustained there for years, and by the time the seal was cast, that whole area was visibly lifeless (see Spera → pre-Spera name "the Cinderlands").

**Important**: Corrupted magic does NOT affect minds directly - not humans, not animals. It does not control thoughts or alter personalities. Animals become aggressive because they are in pain and discomfort from the physical sickness. Psychological effects in humans (paranoia, violence, cruelty) come from human reactions to the physical crisis - sickness, hunger, fear.

### Corrupted Magic Accumulation

**Persistence**: Corrupted magic accumulates indefinitely in the atmosphere. The planet only produces pure magic — it cannot cleanse or neutralize corruption. Pure and corrupted magic mix together in the ambient environment.

**Dilution**: Slow dilution over centuries is possible as the planet continues producing pure magic, but this is uncertain and not understood by people in the current era.

**Current Levels**: The corruption currently in the atmosphere is a tiny fraction of what existed during ancient times. The majority of crystallized corrupted magic is still unmined, sitting untouched in deposits across Spera.

### Mage Sensitivity to Corruption

**Detection**: Mages can detect corrupted magic, but only when present in sufficient concentration. At low levels, it is imperceptible.

**Vulnerability**: Mages are more vulnerable to corruption than ordinary people. When practicing magic, mages must actively absorb surrounding ambient magic to use their abilities and replenish their reserves. This is far more magic-intensive than the passive absorption all beings experience. As a result, mages accumulate corrupted magic in their bodies faster than non-mages.

**Local Mages**: Mages who have lived their entire lives in Spera do not notice the gradual change in quality of ambient magic — they grew up with it slowly worsening and have no baseline for comparison.

**Foreign Mages**: Mages visiting from other nations can sense that something is "wrong" with the ambient magic — a faint trace of something off. However, they will not suffer health consequences without prolonged exposure.

**Timeline of Effects**:
- **Years 1-20**: Low corruption levels, slow accumulation. Toward the end of this period, the most active mages began experiencing slight effects (headaches, tiredness) — easily attributed to other causes. Most mages and the general population noticed nothing.
- **Years 20-35 (present)**: Corruption concentration significantly higher. Most active mages feel real hindrance in their practice. Some mages who practiced intensively throughout the past 35 years now have mid-to-severe health issues (mostly mid, severe cases are rare). Regular mages are only beginning to experience what the most active mages felt 10-15 years ago. Some individuals in the general population are starting to show effects.

**Acceleration**: Corruption accumulation is not linear — it is accelerating. Years 1-20 saw much lower corruption than years 20-35. Now that ambient magic is more concentrated with corruption, effects will manifest faster. The rate at which people become affected is increasing.

### Corruption Magic (Practiced)

- The organism was not present long enough for anyone to learn how to use corrupted magic in any significant way
- People knew it was possible through crude experiments, but that was the extent of it
- Even these crude experiments were enough to cause physical harm to the mages who attempted them
- This never developed into a real discipline or practice

## The Alien Organism

### Origin

- Arrived on the planet via meteorite during the height of the ancient civilization
- The meteorite impact created a deep crater; the Original was at the bottom
- Over millennia, the crater filled in naturally with sediment and rock
- An alien lifeform - not of this world
- Evolved to survive space travel: vacuum, extreme temperatures, absence of magic

### Nature

- Semi-conscious, similar to a fungus or plant
- Not intelligent - does not think, plan, or scheme
- Simply exists, feeds, grows, and reproduces
- The Original is physically indestructible by any means the ancients possessed

### Feeding and Corruption

- **Awakening vs. feeding are different processes**: hibernation ends with exposure to ambient magic of any kind — pure or corrupted, it does not matter, because rousing from hibernation is biological reactivation, not metabolism. Once awake, however, it can only metabolize **pure magic**. Corrupted magic is its own waste product and useless to it as food
- When it consumes pure magic, it excretes corrupted magic as a byproduct
- This is its biological process - not intentional malice
- As it feeds, it saturates surroundings with corrupted magic

### Reproduction

- Reproduces via spores
- Spores grow into new organisms, which feed and release more spores
- Exponential growth pattern

### The Spores vs The Original

**The Spores**:
- NOT indestructible
- Could be killed, but required significant effort and specialized knowledge
- Not everyone could do it
- The problem was volume: too many, spreading too fast, everywhere
- Realistically impossible to eradicate them all
- Spores could be physically touched - they corrupted the magic they fed on, not physical matter directly
- However, proximity to spores caused nausea and illness due to the ambient corrupted magic they excreted

**The Original**:
- Physically indestructible
- Could survive without magic (evolved for space travel)
- When magic vanished, it entered hibernation rather than dying
- The only survivor of its kind on this planet
- Currently dormant

### Current Location

- The Original is entombed at the center of the largest violet crystal formation on the planet
- When the seal activated, the massive concentration of corrupted magic around it crystallized instantly
- It is trapped in crystallized magic - a prison created inadvertently by the seal
- This location is in the neighboring nation that began mining violet crystals
- The formation is currently underground, hidden beneath the filled-in crater
- It has NOT been discovered yet - Spera has been mining other, smaller deposits
- **Why It Remained Undiscovered**: In ancient times, cities were built as far from the Original's lair as possible. Life and development happened elsewhere. As centuries passed, the original reason was forgotten, but the pattern persisted — the area was simply ignored because it held no interest. Cities had developed elsewhere, and this location was just a place filled with nature, not interesting enough to invest in or build upon.
- The largest deposit will be discovered during the story, when mining operations following nearby satellite veins and smaller surrounding deposits accidentally breach into the formation via tunnel

### Awakening Conditions

- Currently in deep hibernation
- The Original requires access to magic to awaken
- Its prison of crystallized corrupted magic provides strong but imperfect insulation from the ambient magic in the atmosphere
- **Two factors drive the awakening:**
  - **Physical approach toward the prison**: Spera's mining operations are removing nearby violet formations, following satellite veins, and digging closer to the prison without realizing what they are approaching. This gradually reduces the surrounding buffer. A direct breach would expose the Original to ambient magic immediately
  - **Atmospheric magic concentration**: even with the prison intact, rising ambient magic levels increase the rate at which magic seeps through its outer layers. The higher the ambient saturation, the faster the Original recovers from hibernation
- For most of the post-Death-of-Magic era, ambient levels were so low that effectively no magic reached the Original. The recent rise — driven primarily by Spera's violet crystal use leaking corrupted magic into the atmosphere — has changed that
- The only practical accelerant is industrial-scale violet processing, which leaks corruption into the atmosphere with every end-use device. Ordinary magic use (mages casting, ancient artifacts being powered) consumes ambient magic rather than releasing it, and so does not contribute to the awakening
- Once awakened, it will produce new spores, beginning a new cycle of exponential corruption

**Timeline**: The discovery of the largest deposit and subsequent mining will occur during the story. The player does not know about the Original or the threat at the start - they will uncover this truth gradually.

## Ancient History

### The Height of Civilization

- Far more advanced than the current era
- Magic was deeply integrated into society
- Technology and capabilities that seem miraculous to modern people
- Quality of life enhanced by abundant magic

### The Arrival and Infestation

- Meteorite brought the Original organism
- It fed, grew, released spores
- Spores spread, each one feeding and releasing more spores
- Corruption spread across large portions of the planet
- By the time the disastrous impact of the organism was discovered, it was already too late - the spores had already spread globally
- Local containment was no longer possible

### Attempts to Stop It

- The ancients tried to kill the spores
- It was possible but required effort and knowledge
- They could not keep up with the spread rate
- They attempted to destroy the Original - nothing worked (indestructible)

**The Cost to Mages:**
- Mages who attempted to destroy the Original or its spores paid a terrible price
- Casting spells in corruption-saturated areas required absorbing that corrupted magic directly into their bodies
- The first mages to confront the Original directly were unaware of this danger — they survived long enough to report back, but died in the days or weeks that followed
- Fighting the infestation was effectively suicide — the people most capable of stopping it were the most vulnerable to it

### The Decision to Seal

**Testing**: The ancients tested on captured spores and confirmed they died without access to magic. This was an informed decision.

**The Delay**: People could not resolve themselves to something so drastic. They spent time looking for a better resolution. During this delay, the civilization continued to decline as corruption spread further.

**Limited Time**: The Original and its spores produced corrupted magic far faster than what occurs with modern violet crystal processing. From meteor impact to the Death of Magic was approximately 2 years. There was not much time to prepare for the aftermath, but the ancients had teleportation, magical communication, and emergency logistics that allowed mass coordination on a timescale impossible in the modern medieval era.

**The Choice**: They knew sealing all magic would cause catastrophe - collapse of their civilization, mass death, a dark age. But there was no other option. The more time passed, the more magic became corrupted and the more civilization suffered. If left to spread further, no one would have survived.

**Multi-Nation Agreement**: The decision was made in agreement with most nations of that era — 5 nations signed the post-Death of Magic pact and agreed on 6 specialized roles for the rebuilding of civilization that would follow. Possibly not all nations of the era agreed. The 6th role (the Keepers) was assigned to a population that did not yet exist as a nation: it would be constructed at the seal-casting hub from the mages who remained behind to cast the spell, plus the otherworlders who stayed with them. See Pact Nation Roles below for the full breakdown.

**The Fate of the Pact Nations**: Most of the original pact nations no longer exist as such. Over the millennia following the Death of Magic, wars, plagues, famine, and the general brutality of the dark age dissolved or transformed most of them. The pact nations were not naive about war - warfare existed in the ancient era - but they did not anticipate that the catastrophe itself would generate the conditions for it. The trauma, hunger, and desperation of the dark age produced conflicts of a scale and ferocity they had no framework to prepare for. Today only a handful of successor states remain, and smaller nations have formed since, some descended from pact fragments, others arising independently.

### Pact Nation Roles

**The Logic of Specialization**: The ancients knew that after the Death of Magic, communication and travel between nations would be extremely difficult — no teleportation, weakened animals, collapsed infrastructure. No single nation could master everything needed to rebuild civilization. Each nation was assigned a specialized role based on the geographic advantages of the territory they would inhabit. Each would develop deep expertise in one domain and share it with the others.

**The Confluence (10-Year Festival)**: To share knowledge between nations, the pact established a gathering every 10 years — known as **The Confluence**. The name evokes rivers from different sources flowing together: each nation brought its specialized knowledge, mixed it with the others, and carried home what the others had developed. It was a massive festival where each nation presented the advancements they had made in their assigned domain. This was not just celebration; it was load-bearing infrastructure for civilization's survival. Techniques, discoveries, and innovations developed by one nation were disseminated to all.

**Location and Organization**: The Confluence was held at a site in the central region of the continent, deliberately chosen so that no nation had a hostile-distance disadvantage in attending. Organization was handled by a standing council of six representatives — one from each pact nation — who coordinated logistics, agendas, and the safe passage of delegations between festivals. Communication during the post-DoM era was difficult, not impossible: messengers traveled by river and road carrying physical letters, and the council's continued existence between festivals was itself one of the pact era's quiet achievements.

**The End**: The Confluence had been weakening for generations before its end — younger generations in the other pact nations had grown indifferent or hostile to the sharing principle, attendance had thinned, and commitment had eroded. Vaeren's invasion of Selvaren (see Vaeren → Why the Shield Turned) was the final shock: when one pact nation destroyed another, the institution lost its premise. The council could not function with a Selvaren seat that no longer existed and a Shield seat occupied by the destroyer. The southwestern fragments consolidated into Aldemar in the same generation, defining the political shape of the dark age that followed. Knowledge transfer stopped with the institution: each surviving nation kept only what it had specialized in, and lost access to the rest.

**The Six Roles**:

| Role | Ancient Name | Specialization | Geographic Basis |
|------|------|---------------|-----------------|
| **The Sowers** | **Maerith** | Agriculture, food production, animal husbandry | Most fertile plains and valleys |
| **The Menders** | **Selvaren** | Medicine, healing, managing magic withdrawal | Region rich in medicinal flora and resources |
| **The Shapers** | **Kaldun** | Construction, tools, metallurgy, engineering | Mineral-rich, mountainous terrain |
| **The Shields** | **Vaeren** | Underground creature containment, fortification, combat techniques | Densest concentration of underground cave exits |
| **The Keepers** | **Iyenor** | Knowledge preservation, archiving history and ancient records | The northern island, chosen as the seal-casters' post-seal destination for isolation and long-term safety |
| **The Mariners** | **Aolanir** | Shipbuilding, navigation, fishing, sea transport | Forested coastline with natural harbors |

**Why These Roles**: Each maps to a critical survival need that could be geographically concentrated. The Sowers needed the best farmland. The Shapers needed mineral deposits. The Shields needed to be where the underground exits were densest. The Mariners needed coastline and timber. The Menders needed access to medicinal resources. The Keepers' role and destination were planned together (see The Keepers' Special Case below).

**The Keepers' Special Case**: Unlike the other five roles, the Keepers were not assigned to an existing population in their pre-seal territory. The pact planners designed the role and the destination together: the ~100 most competent mages who would remain at the hub to cast the seal, plus the few hundred otherworlders who stayed with them, would migrate to the northern island after the spell took effect. The island had been prepared in advance — the most important ancient archives were teleported there before the seal, while teleportation still worked, because moving large archives by river and foot in a post-magic world would have been brutally impractical. The migrants would arrive to find the records already in place, and would develop, over generations, into a culture of preservation. None of these mages or otherworlders were "Keepers" before the assignment — the role and the unified identity crystallized post-seal among a deliberately mixed population. The island was chosen for its natural isolation, which made it an ideal long-term refuge for the archives during the dark age that would follow.

**The Mariners' Critical Function**: With overland travel crippled (weakened animals, no magical transport), sea and river routes became the primary means of moving large quantities of goods between nations. The Mariners' ships carried the Shapers' metal, the Sowers' grain, and the delegates to each festival. They were arguably the connective tissue of the pact.

**Role Survival in the Modern Era**: Most pact roles have been forgotten or corrupted beyond recognition over the millennia. Some nations retain cultural echoes of their original purpose without understanding the origin. The mapping of ancient roles to modern nations is still being determined (see Nations section).

### The Teleportation Hub

**Original Purpose**: A central facility in the mountains, built for inter-nation teleportation. Located in a remote mountain pass, isolated from cities - this isolation meant all available ambient magic in the area could be dedicated to teleportation, which would not have been possible in populated areas where magic was used for many purposes. This made it the natural hub for all inter-nation travel.

**Conversion for the Pact**: When the decision to seal was made, most teleportation devices at the hub were converted into otherworlder summoning portals, to bring as many otherworlders as possible before the seal. Some teleportation devices were kept operational for the evacuation - moving populations to their assigned pact nations.

**Why Centralization Was Essential**:
- **Skill distribution**: All otherworlders arrived at the same place, allowing the ancients to assess their skills and compose balanced groups
- Although same-planet groups were kept together, distribution to nations was done with utility in mind
- Each group sent to a nation was carefully composed to ensure they had enough of each type of knowledge (farmers, healers, builders, etc.)
- If each nation had summoned separately, they would have received random skill distributions — potentially disastrous for survival

**Creating a Safe Zone**:
- The site required a coordinated effort by all nations to make it safe for mages to work. Spores can be destroyed safely with care, but in most of the world they pop up faster than any local force can clear them — clearance loses to spawn rate, and the area degrades again
- The pact concentrated its best mages at the hub specifically to break that ratio. With enough top-tier casters, careful protocols, and time, the zone was fully cleared without losses, and then kept clear through constant monitoring for the duration of the operation
- This allowed mages to build and operate the devices in a stable, corruption-free pocket — something that could not be sustained at any other site, where the spawn rate would have outpaced the clearance work long before the summoning could be completed

**Why Proximity Still Mattered**:
- All living things experienced drawbacks from the lack of magic — not just humans
- Animals bred faster than humans, so new generations without withdrawal effects emerged more quickly
- However, during the immediate aftermath, horses and other beasts of burden were affected and could not carry loads as they would have in the past
- This made long-distance travel difficult, which is why even a central location was better than building devices at the edges of the known world

**The Casting of the Seal**: After populations were evacuated to their designated nations, the Death of Magic spell was cast at the hub. Approximately 100 of the most competent mages remained to perform the spell. A few hundred otherworlders stayed with them - not to help cast, but to help the mages survive the immediate aftermath, as the mages would be struck by magic withdrawal the moment the spell took effect. After the spell was cast, the otherworlders helped the weakened mages descend by river through the mountains to the northern coast, then crossed the sea to the northern island. The full route — river, coastal staging, sea crossing — had been planned and provisioned in advance, with boats and supplies pre-positioned along the way, since post-seal travel logistics could not be improvised.

**The Northern Island Group**: The mages and otherworlders who retreated to the northern island were unique — they were the only group that had not been teleported to their designated nation. They traveled on foot and by river, which meant they were the only people who knew the physical route to the hub and the locations of the summoning devices. This group's otherworlders were the ones who, 5-10 years later, found and destroyed the summoning devices (see The Summoning Devices below). Their knowledge of the hub's location was exclusive, and their descendants form modern Iyenor (the Keepers Nation — see Iyenor under Nations).

**Current State**: The hub is now ancient ruins at the center of the mountain range, overgrown and partially collapsed after millennia. Remains of the teleportation devices can still be found — broken in many parts, covered by vegetation. The site was completely abandoned after the devices were destroyed. Over the centuries, some people came and dismantled pieces for various reasons. Modern people have a vague idea what it was — possibly remembered as "devices used to summon an army against the Ancient Devil" or similar distorted accounts. It is not recognized for what it truly was. This site is distinct from the player's summoning device, which was a prototype built separately.

### The Death of Magic

- Through means now lost, they crystallized ALL magic on the planet
- Pure magic became blue crystals
- Corrupted magic became violet crystals
- All ambient magic vanished
- The spores died (confirmed by prior testing)
- The Original did not die - it entered hibernation

### The Aftermath

- Everything built on magic stopped working
- Infrastructure collapsed
- Famine, disease, social collapse
- Mass death
- Humanity survived but was thrown back to primitive conditions
- Most knowledge was lost
- Thousands of years of slow rebuilding to reach current medieval level

### Magic Withdrawal

The population of the ancient era had absorbed magic their entire lives. When magic vanished, their bodies suffered withdrawal.

**Symptoms:**
- Physical weakness and fatigue
- Mental fog, difficulty concentrating
- Depression and emotional instability
- Chronic pain or discomfort
- More prone to sickness, with more severe symptoms
- Shortened lifespan in severe cases

**Severity**: Most people were not completely debilitated, but they were diminished - easily tired, more vulnerable to illness, bodies struggling to function without magic.

**Duration**: A single generation. Those who experienced the Death of Magic directly took a few years to a decade for their bodies to accommodate and learn to live without magic. Children born after the seal did not experience withdrawal.

**Why Otherworlders Were Essential**: Summoned people from non-magical worlds had never absorbed magic. They did not experience withdrawal. While the native population struggled with diminished capacity, otherworlders were fully functional - healthy workers who could help maintain society during the crisis.

### Lost Knowledge and Archives

**What Remains**: Ancient ruins stand as testament to the old civilization's might. Archives and records exist in various locations.

**Non-Magical Writings**: Some non-magical writings were left in people's hands after the Death of Magic, and some were written afterward. However, most have been lost to time, and those that remain cannot be read due to the language barrier.

**Why It's Inaccessible**: Ancient knowledge is double-locked:
1. **Language barrier**: The Ancient language has been lost to time. No one can read the texts.
2. **Magic barrier**: Archives require active magic input to open — the seals are mechanisms that only respond to deliberate magical manipulation, not passive barriers.

**Why the Language Was Lost**: The otherworlders summoned during the Death of Magic were taught the Ancient language through the summoning process, but they still spoke their own native languages. Otherworlders came from different non-magical worlds, though not a huge number of worlds — some groups were summoned from the same world and stayed together.

These people were unwillingly summoned to another world. They could not return home — without magic, the summoning devices were useless. Preserving their language and culture was the least they could do. Since going back was impossible, they opted for the second-best thing: making this place feel like home.

Before the Death of Magic, both summoning and ancient magic supported universal language comprehension. After the seal, no new otherworlders arrived, magic-based translation vanished, and the last universally gifted generations eventually died. Ancient did not disappear immediately. It survived for generations, especially among mages, scholars, and Keepers, but it gradually became less useful than the living languages spoken by mixed native-otherworlder populations.

Since many natives were weakened by magic withdrawal, otherworlders became some of the most important and influential people of their time. Over generations, the true Ancient language was modified beyond recognition and eventually lost — competing with multiple otherworlder languages, and lacking practical use (the archives were inaccessible anyway). Over centuries and then millennia, it became like Latin without a living priesthood strong enough to preserve fluency: studied in fragments, quoted in rituals, visible in old texts, but no longer truly understood.

**The Dark Centuries**: In the centuries immediately following the Death of Magic, archives were completely inaccessible — there was literally no magic to work with. Even if someone could somehow read the language, no one could use magic to open the sealed archives. This period lasted centuries, possibly a millennium.

**Modern Attempts**: As magic gradually returned, some mages managed to open archives over the centuries. However, many archives have been damaged or lost to time — millennia of neglect, wars, natural decay, and deliberate destruction during the dark age took their toll. Scholars have been studying the Ancient language, and some words or context can now be understood at a surface level. However, this understanding remains too fragmentary to extract meaningful knowledge from complex texts.

**Current Understanding**: People know a great civilization existed and fell. They do not know how or why. Various theories exist, but nothing is certain. The truth — scattered across surviving archives that can now be physically opened but not read — awaits someone who can understand the Ancient language.

### Knowledge Degradation Over Time

**Ancient Era**: People understood the distinction between pure and corrupted magic. They knew blue crystals were crystallized pure magic and violet crystals were crystallized corrupted magic.

**Post-Death of Magic**: With no ambient magic, no one could use either type of crystal. The practical distinction became irrelevant — both were inert, useless stones.

**Over Millennia**: Without practical relevance, the "why" behind the distinction was forgotten. Only vague warnings survived. Written records were mostly lost — they were in the Ancient language, and no one saw practical importance in preserving warnings about stones they couldn't use anyway. The first generations after the Death of Magic may have cared, but subsequent generations did not, and the knowledge faded.

**Modern Understanding**: Before the processing breakthrough, people simply thought violet crystals were a variant of blue crystals that they hadn't figured out how to use yet. The two look similar enough that this assumption was reasonable.

### The Iyenor Monoliths

Faced with the slow erosion of paper archives, the loss of fluent Ancient speakers, and the geographic fragility of any single repository, the Keepers of Iyenor proposed during the Confluence era a more durable form of preservation: **stone monoliths inscribed in Ancient**, erected at sites across the world so that critical knowledge would exist in many places at once and could not be lost to a single fire, war, or shipwreck.

**Why Stone**: Paper degrades, archives can be sacked, and the island itself was a single point of failure. Stone outlasts civilizations. Even if no one alive could read what was carved, the carvings would still be there centuries later, waiting.

**Distribution**: Monoliths were placed in many nations during the centuries the Confluence still functioned. Iyenor sent emissaries who traveled with the carvers, selected sites, taught local custodians what little they could about the importance of the markers, and then moved on. This was a generations-long undertaking — not a single project but a continuous diplomatic and logistical effort sustained as long as the pact era held together.

**Modern State**: Most monoliths are gone. War, deliberate destruction, weathering, and the simple fact that nobody could read them and many came to view them as pagan oddities all took their toll. Some were repurposed as building stone. Some were defaced. A handful survive, in varying conditions — some intact and legible, some half-buried, some broken into fragments scattered across a ruin.

**Why They Matter Now**: A surviving monolith is one of the few places a modern person can encounter Ancient text in the wild, outside the sealed archives of Iyenor. For the player — who can read Ancient — each surviving monolith is a recoverable fragment of lost knowledge, encountered as part of exploring the world rather than requiring a journey to the island.

### Names and Naming (Ancient Era)

Names from the pre-Death of Magic civilization draw from a broad palette unique to that culture. They are rooted in a language lineage that existed before any otherworlder arrived on the planet and do not map to any real Earth naming tradition.

**Shared qualities across the palette:**
- Old, weathered, culturally rooted
- Distinctly non-Earth in feel — the reader should not instinctively reach for "French," "Greek," "Hebrew," or any other recognizable real-world tradition
- Evocative of a civilization that lasted over a millennium, allowing for regional and generational variation

**Stylistic directions that all fit within this palette:**
- **Soft & flowing**: Vaeren, Sellis, Iyen, Ohara, Alira
- **Rooted & consonant-rich**: Therek, Kaldun, Orvan, Maerith
- **Vowel-heavy, old-feeling**: Aolan, Iora, Ulya, Evael

Any of these styles can appear. A civilization spanning ~1024 years would have multiple regional variations, historical fashions, and generational shifts.

**Modern names** (used in the current era) have a different aesthetic — they descend from otherworlder languages that arrived around the time of the Death of Magic, drifted through roughly 2,000 years of linguistic evolution. Importantly, otherworlders were summoned from **multiple non-magical worlds, not only Earth**, and some of those worlds may not have been inhabited by humans at all. This means the modern naming pool is genuinely heterogeneous:

- Some names descend from Earth languages of ~2,000 years ago and can feel half-familiar (names like Marek, Iula, Theris) without being exact matches
- Other names descend from languages of other non-magical human worlds — similar register to Earth names but not traceable to any real culture
- A portion may descend from non-human otherworlder languages, producing names that feel genuinely foreign to any Earth ear

All of these have been churning together for ~2,000 years of mixing, drift, and evolution. Modern naming conventions will be developed in more detail separately.

**Note**: The existence of non-human otherworlders has implications beyond naming (modern population ancestry, visible demographics, cultural memory). Flagged in Open Questions for later resolution.

### Surviving Oral Tradition

Despite the loss of written knowledge, some memories survived through oral tradition - though warped by thousands of years of retelling.

**What Death of Magic Era Survivors Tried to Preserve:**
- A terrible monster destroyed the old world
- The monster's lair became covered in violet crystal after the catastrophe
- Violet crystals appeared in places where corruption was worst
- Violet was linked to corruption and to the monster

**What Actually Survived (Modern Era):**
- The specific details above degraded into vague, childlike tales over millennia
- No actionable warnings remain - just fuzzy folklore
- The color violet is associated with bad luck, ill omens, or misfortune
- Stories to frighten children, not serious historical knowledge
- No one knows WHERE the monster's lair was, or what specifically to avoid
- Most educated people consider these stories primitive myths with no factual basis

**How Warnings Were Dismissed:**
- When early violet crystal processing experiments showed no immediate harm, scholars dismissed the old warnings as baseless superstition
- The gradual accumulation of harm let people rationalize: "If it were truly dangerous, we'd have seen it immediately"
- By the time the warnings proved true, the neighboring nation was already dependent on violet crystal energy

### The Underground Threat — The Hollows

**Name**: The creatures that emerge from the underground are collectively known as **The Hollows**. The name has been worn smooth by millennia of use — its original meaning is debated and largely forgotten. It evokes multiple things at once: the hollow places they come from (caves, depressions, the empty spaces beneath the earth), the unsettling sense that something is *missing* from them in a way no one can quite articulate, and the resonant, echoing sounds they make moving through stone tunnels. People in the Shield nation simply call them the Hollows the way one names something too familiar to embellish.

**Nature**: Beneath the continent lies an extensive network of deep cave systems harboring an entire subterranean ecosystem. Magic density increases with depth — the closer to the planet's core, the denser the ambient magic. As a result, creatures dwelling deeper underground are more magical and more dangerous, while those near the surface are largely non-magical and less threatening. The underground is a gradient from mundane to extraordinarily dangerous.

**Ancient Management**: In ancient times, mages sealed all dangerous exits from the underground — the deep access points where powerful, magical creatures could surface. However, some entrances remained open and regulated, used for hunting and gathering rare materials from the creatures within. Venturing into the upper levels of the caves was a known, managed activity — dangerous but profitable.

**Post-Death of Magic**: When magic vanished, the magical wards on sealed exits broke. The deepest, most magic-dependent creatures did not die. Without ambient magic to sustain their bodies, many entered a state of deep slumber or biological suspension. The non-magical and less magic-dependent creatures of the upper and mid levels remained active. With the wards gone, these creatures began surfacing freely through exits across the continent.

**Why They Cannot Be Eradicated**: The cave systems extend deeper than anyone can explore. The subterranean ecosystem is vast and self-sustaining. Humans can only manage the surface exits — they cannot invade the underground world and eliminate the source population. The creatures surface for food, territory, and breeding cycles. It is persistent animal behavior — endless, like tides.

**Distribution**: Every nation has some cave exits to manage, but one region sits atop the densest concentration — essentially the roof of the largest underground network. This became the basis for the Shield nation's pact role (see Pact Nation Roles).

**Modern Era**: As magic has slowly returned (~50% of ancient levels), the underground ecosystem is shifting again. Deeper creatures that were dormant or weakened are regaining strength. Some mages have helped re-seal certain exits, but the threat remains very much present. The underground gradient of magical density means that as ambient magic continues to rise, increasingly dangerous creatures may begin surfacing again.

### Modern Hollow Breaks

**Not a new phenomenon**: Hollow Breaks have occurred throughout the centuries since the Death of Magic. Containing the worst of them was the original basis of the Shield role — Vaeren has dealt with breaks continuously since the wards failed, and other nations have faced their own across the millennia. A "Hollow Break" refers to a specific incident where one or more underground exits become active beyond what local defenses can routinely manage.

**Severity varies**: Many breaks are contained quickly — local garrisons and troops from nearby villages can together suppress a surge before it spreads, and the area recovers. Others overwhelm regional defenses, in which case the surrounding zone is progressively abandoned until the break can be sealed or pushed back. The Aldemar Break (see below) sits at the worse end of that spectrum.

**What is changing now**: As ambient magic levels continue to rise, the underground ecosystem is stirring more broadly. Breaks are becoming more frequent and more severe, and deeper, more ancient Hollows that had been dormant or weakened during the magic-poor centuries are beginning to awaken. Some are bold enough to surface through exits that had been quiet for generations — including exits in nations like Aldemar that historically saw little Hollow activity.

**The Aldemar Break**: Approximately 1-2 years before the player's arrival, a Hollow Break occurred within Aldemar. The affected zone has been progressively abandoned as it grows month by month. The cause is consistent with the broader pattern — rising magic stirring deeper, bolder Hollows in numbers Aldemar had never needed to manage.

**Why the Response Has Been Inadequate**: Aldemar historically had relatively few Hollow exits, so it never needed to maintain serious anti-Hollow expertise. Knowledge that once flowed through the Confluence has decayed in places that didn't face the threat regularly. They are now learning on the job, with fragmented institutional memory. Adding insult to injury: the nation that retained the deepest Hollow expertise — Vaeren — has cold, centuries-old enmity with Aldemar, making cross-border knowledge requests politically fraught.

**Current State**:
- The inhabitable zone is still growing, month over month
- Refugees displaced from the affected region have arrived in surrounding areas, including the village where the player begins
- Serious plans are now being formulated to address the situation more aggressively
- Soldiers and mages visibly travel through the village toward the affected area — heading to the front, returning from it, or coordinating en route

## The Crystals

### Using Crystals

- Using crystals to power anything requires ambient magic to exist
- Crystals cannot be used in isolation - there must be magic in the environment to interface with them
- In the centuries immediately following the Death of Magic, crystals were useless - there was no ambient magic to work with
- This is why the ancients could not use crystallized magic to sustain their economy after the seal
- **Raw crystals are inert.** Both blue and violet crystals must be mined AND processed into a usable form before any device can draw on them. Processing requires specialized facilities and is costly — this is what makes crystal-powered devices a luxury rather than a commodity in most of the world
- Crystal-powered devices fall into two broad categories:
  - **Crafted artifacts with fixed functions** — lanterns that light without oil, healers' diagnostic instruments, enchanted blades, sealed lockboxes. These let non-mages access magical effects through preset, single-purpose mechanisms
  - **Mage batteries** — artifacts designed to amplify a mage's potency. The mage absorbs energy from the battery (drawn from the crystal feeding it) and channels it through their own casting, effectively expanding their mana pool. A sufficiently charged battery also lets a mage feed extra energy into ancient artifacts that no single modern mage could power on their own (see Ancient Artifacts under The Modern Era)
- **Blue and violet crystals are not interchangeable.** A device designed for blue crystals will not accept violet, and vice versa — the two energies are not cross-compatible. Each crystal type has its own family of devices, built around its specific properties. Spera's processing facilities and violet-compatible end-use artifacts are a separate category from the blue-crystal-powered devices found in the rest of the world

### Corrupted Magic and Devices

- Magical devices (artifacts, summoning devices, etc.) can function with corrupted magic
- Corrupted magic harms living beings and the environment, not mechanical devices
- A device powered by corrupted magic works normally - the harm is to the surroundings, not the device itself

### Blue Crystals (Pure Magic)

- Crystallized pure magic from the seal event
- Safe energy source — processing and use produce no environmental side effects
- Found scattered across the continent; one can occasionally stumble upon a deposit
- Extraction techniques have been understood for centuries. Blue-crystal-powered devices are well-known but uncommon outside major cities — processing is expensive and supply is scattered rather than concentrated

### Violet Crystals (Corrupted Magic)

- Crystallized corrupted magic from the seal event
- 2 to 5 times more potent than blue crystals as energy (potency varies)
- Concentrated in areas where the infestation was worst during ancient times
- The largest deposit is the prison of the Original organism
- **Unprocessed violet crystals are inert** — they were even used as jewelry in Spera before the breakthrough
- For thousands of years, no one could process violet crystals. Spera's breakthrough 35 years ago finally unlocked their use
- **Every end-use violet device leaks corruption.** Raw violet crystals are inert, but corruption is released when violet crystal energy is converted into usable output by violet-compatible devices. This means lamps, heaters, mage batteries, utility infrastructure, household artifacts, and other violet devices all leak small amounts of corrupted magic while used. Industrial facilities and dense cities are worse because they concentrate thousands of devices in one place, but ordinary citizens using violet energy also contribute.
- Spera's industrial scale of violet use is driven by economics, not just supply: violet's 2–5x potency over blue, combined with massive concentrated deposits in Spera's territory, made utility-scale infrastructure economically justified once processing was solved. Blue crystals — even in concentrated deposits — don't carry the same case. Per-crystal output is too low to justify scaling beyond artifact-level use; conventional energy sources fill the same role at lower cost. So far as anyone knows

## The Modern Era

### Current State

**Technology Level**: High Medieval
- Castles, knights, established kingdoms
- Guilds and structured trades
- Steel weapons and armor

**Magic's Return**: The planet has been producing magic again over millennia. Ambient levels are slowly rising. Magic users have reappeared, though still rare. Current ambient magic levels are roughly 50% of what existed during ancient times — significant enough for mages to practice and crystals to function, but far below the saturation of the old era.

**Ancient Artifacts**:
- Ancient artifacts were designed to draw power from ambient magic and from direct mage injection. They were not built to interface with crystals — there is no socket, no slot, no mechanism for a crystal to feed an ancient artifact directly
- Ancient mages had more mana available than modern mages and could inject more into artifacts on their own
- Over centuries, modern researchers experimented with old artifacts — injecting their own magic and getting partial results (ambient magic today is still below ancient levels, and modern mages have less personal mana than ancient ones)
- This is how people learned the artifacts worked with magic, but a single modern mage rarely has enough magic to power one alone
- **Two practical workarounds exist** for operating ancient artifacts in the modern era:
  - **Multi-mage collaboration**: several mages pool their input into one artifact, combining their reserves
  - **Mage batteries**: the mage uses a crystal-charged battery to top up their reserves on the fly, then channels that boosted energy into the artifact. The crystal does not power the artifact directly — it powers the mage, who powers the artifact
- In either case, the chain still ends with a mage feeding an ancient artifact. Crystals cannot bypass that step

### Discovery of Violet Crystals

**What Was Known:**
- The violet crystal formations have been documented and known for centuries
- They were recognized as different from blue crystals, but nobody could use them

**The Breakthrough:**
- The recent "discovery" was a processing breakthrough - a method to prepare violet crystals so violet-compatible devices could convert their energy into usable output
- This breakthrough occurred 35 years ago
- As with all periods of time, researchers had been trying to solve energy issues and improve quality of life - such experiments eventually yielded success
- This breakthrough unlocked access to a power source 2-5x more potent than blue crystals

**Mining Timeline:**
- Violet crystals are abundant throughout the nation — there was never a need to actively survey for more deposits
- The first years after the breakthrough were spent developing infrastructure: manufacturing mines, equipment, and processing facilities
- Mining started slowly and became more efficient over time
- 35 years of gradual expansion has brought operations close to discovering the largest deposit (the Original's prison)

**Prototype Exports:**
- The processing technique remains a closely guarded secret of Spera
- However, some prototype violet-compatible devices have been sold to wealthy foreign nobles
- These devices saw a warm welcome from those rich enough to afford them
- Due to low local use over a short time, no visible health incidents have occurred outside Spera from these exports

**What the Energy Is Used For:**
- This is NOT industrial in the sense of steam engines or heavy machinery
- Violet crystal energy powers utilities (light, heat), agricultural infrastructure (heated greenhouses, irrigation pumps, growing-season extension in Spera's harsher northern climate), and Spera's family of violet-compatible devices, including violet-powered mage batteries. Violet does not power blue-compatible devices — Spera built its own ecosystem of artifacts and infrastructure around its crystal type. Through their violet-powered mage batteries, mages can in turn channel energy into ancient artifacts that no single modern mage could power alone
- Spera became dependent on this energy for basic survival needs - heating homes, lighting cities, and feeding a population grown larger than its arable land could support without violet-assisted agriculture

**Why They Can't Stop:**
- The nation's population grew during the 35 years of violet crystal usage
- The prosperity triggered immigration into Spera from poorer border regions and from people seeking work, safety, or a second chance
- Over 35 years, Spera rebuilt daily life around violet energy. Stopping now would feel less like giving up a luxury and more like cutting electricity, heating, transport, and food infrastructure overnight
- Stopping extraction would cause catastrophic consequences without external help — famine in regions dependent on violet-powered agriculture, deaths from cold in areas relying on violet crystal heating
- The population has grown beyond what conventional energy sources can sustain
- This is an energy crisis, not villainy - an Oppenheimer-style tragedy
- They unlocked a power source they cannot easily live without, but which is actively poisoning the world

### The Crisis

**What Happened**:
- Mining extracts violet crystals (the extraction itself is not the primary problem)
- Converting violet crystal energy into usable output is what releases corrupted magic
- This conversion happens locally in every end-use violet device - in homes, cities, wherever violet energy is used for heat, light, mage batteries, utilities, or other artifacts
- People fell ill, animals became aggressive, crops failed
- Effects spread from population centers and usage points throughout the nation, not from the mines

**Why It Wasn't Stopped**:
- Early researchers did not know violet crystals were dangerous - small-scale use released too little corrupted magic to cause noticeable harm
- The danger emerged gradually over years of widespread usage as corrupted magic accumulated in the atmosphere
- Health effects began appearing perhaps 15-20 years ago, with gradual increase over time
- By the time patterns emerged and people began to have doubts, the nation was already dependent on violet crystal energy
- This was not immediate knowledge followed by greed - it was slow realization followed by economic and infrastructural inertia
- The corruption is insidious: ordinary citizens using violet energy to heat their homes or light their streets all contribute to the problem

**Current State of Spera**:
- Health issues appearing in the population, especially among active mages
- Growing internal debate about whether violet crystals are the cause
- Researchers being called in to investigate
- Most citizens are not yet alarmed enough to take drastic action
- A rare few have left the country

**Cross-Border Spread**:
- Corrupted magic released into the atmosphere gradually spreads beyond Spera's borders
- Carried by wind and weather patterns over the years
- After 35 years of accumulation, neighboring nations are beginning to experience effects

**The Conflict**:
- Vaeren is in tense conflict with Spera because they believe the spreading corruption is affecting their cities
- War has not yet been officially declared at the story's start - the situation is at the tension/negotiation phase
- War may be officially declared during the game
- Vaeren's religious beliefs (violet crystals as "Tears of the Ancient Devil") add a moral/religious dimension to their objections
- Spera dismisses these concerns as superstition and unproven accusations
- Extraction continues

**Why They Won't Stop**:
- Stopping extraction means losing the conflict, losing the means to sustain the country, losing power
- Those responsible argue that the crystals are NOT causing the problems
- They deny the connection between extraction and the degradation of health, crops, and land
- Official position: the corruption has other causes; the crystals are safe and essential

### Aldemar

- Aldemar is not at war and village life still functions, but the country is under growing pressure from an active Hollow Break
- Refugees, soldiers, and mages pass through parts of the country, including the village region where the player begins
- Not yet directly affected by corruption - the major violet crystal usage centers are in Spera
- Aware of tensions between Spera and Vaeren, but not directly involved
- See the full Aldemar entry under the Nations section

## Calendar System

The world has used two calendars across its history, divided by the single greatest rupture in its timeline: the Death of Magic. Archive fragments should always be dated in whichever calendar was in use at the time of writing.

### The Two Calendars

| Calendar | Span | Reference Point | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ancient Calendar** | Year 1 → Year 1024 | TBD (rooted in the ancient civilization's founding history) | ~1024 years; ends with the Death of Magic |
| **Modern Calendar** | Year 1 → Year 2312 (present) | The Death of Magic itself — the year the seal was cast, after which the world began counting again | ~2312 years so far; currently in use |

**In-world naming and what Year 1 commemorates**: Within the world, Year 1 of the Modern Calendar marks a long-ago catastrophe that everyone counts from — but the *fact* of the reset is universal while the *understanding* of what it commemorates is not. Modern people know the world had a great rupture in deep history and that magic was much stronger before it. The deliberate, planned nature of the Death of Magic — that it was a sacrifice to seal the Original — is the deep mystery the player slowly uncovers. Different cultures use different names for the era: working candidates include "After the Fall," "The Silent Year," "When the Gods Left," or regional/older variants. No single official name dominates, and the player may hear several across their travels.

### Structure

- **12 months**, same names and order as Earth (January through December) — this is a deliberate simplification for player readability
- **~30 days per month**, 365-day year
- In-world, we can assume the ancient civilization developed a calendar that happens to align with ours — no in-game explanation is needed

### Key Dates

- **Meteor impact**: approximately 2 years before the Death of Magic (Ancient Calendar Year 1022)
- **Death of Magic**: Ancient Calendar Year 1024 — final year of that calendar / Modern Calendar Year 1
- **Confluence collapses**: Modern Calendar ~Year 500, when the pact era visibly ended with the formation of Aldemar
- **Current era**: Modern Calendar **Year 2312**
- **Game over (if nothing is done)**: approximately Modern Calendar Year 2315 — when the countdown reaches zero and the Original awakens

### Real-Time Spans

- **Death of Magic to present**: 2,312 years
- **Ancient Calendar duration**: ~1,024 years
- **Modern Calendar duration so far**: ~2,312 years

These final two years before the Death of Magic still unfolded with terrifying speed. Ancient civilization had high magic, teleportation, rapid communication, and emergency logistics, so decisions and mass movement could happen much faster than a modern medieval society could manage. Even so, it was a tight window for everything that had to happen: realization, failed direct attempts on the Original, the decision to seal, hub conversion, mass summoning, pact negotiation, role assignment, population evacuation, and the casting itself.

### Dating Archive Fragments

- **Pre-DoM fragments** → dated in the Ancient Calendar. The ancients kept rigorous records; dates are precise and reliable.
- **Post-DoM fragments** → dated in the Modern Calendar. Records from the early dark age (roughly Modern Years 1–500) are often approximate, contested, or unknown — the calendar's count was preserved as a symbolic anchor by the Keepers and the surviving pact mages, but routine record-keeping collapsed for centuries across most of the continent. Dates from this period frequently rely on guesswork, context, or physical analysis of the materials.
- Fragments written across the Ancient → Modern transition (or in the immediate decades after the seal) may be ambiguous about which system they use — this is a feature, not a bug.

Format example: **"Year 1021 of the [Ancient Calendar Name], August 14th"** — a fragment written shortly before the meteor impact.

### Calendar Names (TBD)

The Ancient Calendar still needs a proper in-world name. For the Modern Calendar, the working assumption is that no single official name dominates — different cultures use different terms (see *In-world naming* above), and the player encounters several variants in the field.

The northern island group that later destroyed the known summoning devices did not know this prototype existed.

The village the player starts in did not yet exist at the time — the surrounding region was empty for thousands of years before settlers eventually arrived, by which point the device was already overgrown and indistinguishable from countless other ancient ruins

### Otherworlder Abilities

**Broad Language Comprehension**: The summoning process grants the ability to understand and speak all languages. This was an imperative — otherworlders were summoned from multiple different worlds and spoke entirely different languages from each other, not just from the natives. Without universal comprehension, otherworlders could not communicate with natives, with each other, or coordinate the survival effort. The summoning imbued this ability automatically.

**Ancient Language — The Unique Gift**: This broad comprehension includes the Ancient language, which is now extinct. Modern inhabitants cannot read it — the language was lost over millennia as otherworlder languages competed with and eventually replaced it. The player is one of the only people alive who can read Ancient texts, a direct and unique consequence of the summoning process.

**Magic Affinity**: Like all otherworlders, the player has natural sensitivity to magic due to coming from a non-magical world (see Magic System > Otherworlders and Magic).

**Why This Matters Now**: The player is potentially the first person in millennia who can both read Ancient texts AND access magically-sealed archives. They could unlock knowledge that has been inaccessible since the Death of Magic.

### Being Discovered

People will eventually deduce the player is an otherworlder from old tales. The signs:
- Strange behavior (doesn't know basic customs or common knowledge)
- Can read Ancient script that no one else understands
- Has magic affinity as an adult with zero training

Any one trait is odd. All three together points to one conclusion.

### The Way Home

**The Goal**: The player wants to return to their world, their home.

**The Portal**: The summoning device/portal that brought the player is located in the forest. After millennia, it is covered by roots, trees, and vegetation - almost not visible anymore. It does not currently function. The villagers know there is *some kind of* old ruin out in the forest — generations have passed it on hunts and gathering trips, and at some distant point in history people likely tried to make it do something and failed — but no one alive in the village understands what it is or pays it any particular attention. It is just one more weathered remnant of the lost civilization, unremarkable in a world full of them.

**The Path**: The exact method to return home is not yet known to the player. Possibilities may involve reverse-engineering the portal, discovering new magic, or finding Ancient knowledge about the summoning process.

### The Temptation

At some point in the story, an important figure from Spera will approach the player. They will offer to help the player find a way home. This creates moral conflict - potentially accepting help from those causing the world's crisis in exchange for achieving a personal goal.

### Arrival Circumstances

- Appears in a forest, at night, during rain
- Completely disoriented and frightened
- Possibly brief glimpse of their normal life just before transportation (so the player understands what happened)

### Initial Situation

- Attacked by a low-level creature
- Clearly outmatched and terrified
- Rescued by guards who heard the commotion

### How They're Perceived

- Guards see their visible disorientation and fear
- The player's inability to explain themselves ("I have no idea what's going on")
- Their struggle against a low-level threat proves they're not dangerous
- The guards focus on the player's behavior and state, not their strange clothing
- The village is not a war zone, but Aldemar is under pressure from an active Hollow Break. Guards are alert, yet they are also used to displaced, frightened, confused people arriving from unsafe roads and abandoned settlements. This makes the player unusual, but not automatically treated as an enemy.
- Guards conclude: a lost, confused person in need of help — not a threat

### Status

- Brought to the village
- Placed in the care of a local caretaker — someone who helps people in need (lost travelers, the displaced, those who fall through the cracks)
- The caretaker serves as tutorial, helping the player learn how things work
- Player must earn their keep

### The Countdown

- A countdown is displayed in the game UI, starting at approximately 3 years (subject to tuning)
- **Visible to the player, not to the character**: The countdown exists only in the interface. The player character does not see it, sense it, or know it exists. This creates deliberate dramatic irony — the player feels the dread of an impending deadline while the character lives in ignorance until the story reveals what is at stake
- Because one in-game day is roughly 6,000 steps, a three-year countdown represents long-term pressure rather than an immediate time attack
- At the start, the player does not know what the countdown represents or what happens when it reaches zero
- The countdown is tied to the Original's awakening — when it reaches zero, the Original breaks free, produces new spores, and the world ends. Modern mages are not capable enough to replicate the Death of Magic spell or neutralize the Original
- The player discovers what the countdown means gradually through the story
- **Baseline tick**: The countdown ticks down passively at a baseline rate driven by Spera's ongoing violet crystal processing and mining operations — these continue regardless of the player. Doing nothing does not freeze the timer; the world's existing trajectory is already pushing toward the awakening
- **Player modulation**: Player choices and actions can accelerate or decelerate that baseline. The countdown is NOT fixed
- **What moves the countdown**: anything that affects either of the two awakening drivers (see The Alien Organism → Awakening Conditions). Player actions only register when they shift demand or supply at scale — a single person using a violet lantern is a rounding error on a continent's atmosphere. The meaningful levers are choices that change violet adoption village-wide, region-wide, or nation-wide, or that affect the mining approach to the prison. Things that *accelerate* it: helping spread violet crystal technology into new markets or settlements, supporting Spera's industrial expansion, contributing to demand growth, or aiding mining operations near the prison. Things that *slow* it: destroying or disrupting violet mines and processing facilities, helping factions opposed to extraction, organizing communities or regions to refuse or abandon violet energy, or any direct interference with the mining approach to the prison
- This is the core tension mechanic: visible, urgent stakes whose meaning unfolds over time

### Visual Transition

- The player arrives in modern clothing from their world
- The caretaker provides the player with local clothing
- This serves as the player's first "equipment" upgrade
- Marks the visual transition into the world - the player now looks like they belong

## The Opening Sequence

1. (Optional) Brief glimpse of player's normal life in their world — walking in the street, sitting at their computer, an ordinary moment cut short
2. Sudden transportation — player appears in the forest at night, in rain, with thunder. The portal that brought them hums slowly into silence behind them
3. Disorientation and fear — player tries to wake themselves up, looks around lost, quickly becomes soaked
4. Strange sounds in the dark; player runs, stumbles, falls. Wet and dirty now
5. A creature emerges and attacks. The player is clearly outmatched and cries for help
6. Guards arrive, drawn by the noise, and dispatch the creature. They are already patrolling because of the Aldemar Hollow Break: although the village is not close to the break, villages and cities have adopted prevention measures because some Hollows can travel far from the affected zone
7. Guards interrogate the player. Seeing their confused and panicked state, and hearing them keep asking what's happening and where they are, the guards take note of the player's name — it sounds foreign to them — and discuss between themselves. They conclude the player is probably a refugee from a village to the north that was hit during the Hollow Break: someone who has lost everything and run blindly south. This becomes the player's *de facto* cover identity for entering the village — built by the guards themselves, accepted by everyone, requiring no lie from the player
8. Guards escort the player to the village and let them sleep in a temporary building for the night
9. The next morning, the player wakes to **Aelis** — the young woman who helps the displaced. She is the first face they see in this world by daylight
10. Tutorial begins under her guidance — earning keep, learning village life, learning how this world works

### Prologue / Act 1 Direction

- The early plot is centered on Aldemar's Hollow Break, not on Spera or the Original. The village still functions, but the wider country has changed posture: patrols, warnings, refugee movement, soldiers and mages passing through, and standing orders to kill wandering Hollows on sight
- The player is pulled into a local survival-and-belonging story first: finding footing, earning keep, learning village life, and understanding why everyone is tense
- The player will hear about Spera's violet-crystal technology during Act 1 before understanding its true danger. It should first appear as distant news, rumor, or something carried by a traveler or visitor from Spera
- Crystal technology should be introduced in contrast: first, the player sees menders/healers using blue-crystal artifacts in an accepted, useful way; later, the player sees someone use a violet-crystal artifact, establishing that Spera's devices feel related but distinct
- The portal/arrival site can be found through optional exploration rather than as a required quest. It is deeply overgrown and easy to miss. There is no meaningful explanation written on the portal itself, but a small ancient stone nearby may simply read "Welcome" - a quiet remnant of the guidance that should have existed for summoned arrivals

### Continental Geography

### Aldemar

- A pact-fragment fusion nation. Not a direct successor to any single original pact nation — Aldemar is what consolidation produced
- **Composition**: Sower-core (descended primarily from Maerith), with absorbed Mariner fragments (Aolanir) along the coast and minor Shaper inheritance (Kaldun) from the mountain frontier with Spera. None of these original nations exists today as such
- **Founding pressure**: Forged defensively in the dark age against the rising military aggression of Vaeren (the Shield successor). Vaeren faced an existential Hollow surge when the wards broke at the Death of Magic and over-militarized to survive. That militarization eventually scaled past defense and pressured neighbors, leaving the smaller pact-fragments adjacent to it no choice but to consolidate. Aldemar is the result
- **The geography of that war**: Vaeren is northeast and Aldemar is southwest, on opposite ends of the continent. Vaeren's expansion reached the southwestern pact-fragments by pushing through what would only centuries later become Spera — at the time, an unsettled, abandoned no-man's-land known to outsiders as **the Cinderlands** (see Spera under Nations), which no surviving pact nation claimed. The feared portion of that region was the specific area around the Original's lair, not the whole territory, so Vaeren's armies could move through the empty northern and southern stretches without contesting it. Mountain passes and coastal sea routes were also used. The geographic distance did not protect the southwest; it only delayed the pressure long enough for the surviving fragments to recognize the threat and consolidate
- **The trigger — Selvaren's fall**: The proximate cause of the alliance was Vaeren's invasion and destruction of **Selvaren**, the original Mender nation. The invasion did not come from nowhere — Vaeren had been visibly straining for generations under its uniquely brutal conditions, and Selvaren had tried repeatedly to raise those concerns at the Confluence without the other nations taking them seriously (see Vaeren → Why the Shield Turned). When the Shield finally broke and seized Selvaren's safer lands, watching one pact nation erase another pact nation was the wake-up call: if the Shield could end the Menders, no smaller fragment would survive alone. Selvaren refugees scattered; some carried medical knowledge into the Maerith-Aolanir-Kaldun fragments that became Aldemar, but the institutional depth of the Mender tradition did not survive — which is part of why modern medicine across the world is shallower than its ancient inheritance would suggest
- The name **Aldemar** is provisional — may be revised as the nation's identity is refined further
- Located in the southwest, south of the mountain range
- Where the player arrives and starts
- Aldemar is not at war and ordinary village life still functions, but the country is under growing pressure from an active Hollow Break
- Refugees, soldiers, and mages visibly move through affected regions and nearby villages
- Not yet directly affected by corruption — the major violet crystal usage centers are in Spera
- Aware of the tensions between Spera and Vaeren, but not directly involved
- Old, cold enmity with Vaeren persists as cultural memory from the founding war, but is no longer hot — the two nations have not been actively at war for a long time
- Contains: Village, Forest, Mine, Fisherman's Cabin (current locations)

**Geography:**
- A mountain range separates Aldemar from Spera, acting as a natural border
- This geographic barrier helps explain why corruption has not spread significantly into Aldemar

### Spera (The Violet Nation)

- **Not a pact nation** - a completely separate origin
- **Name origin**: "Spera" means hope. Named only a few hundred years ago - the first nation name of its kind, not derived from ancient pact-era naming conventions. The name was chosen by the first great groups who attempted to unite the scattered people hiding within this land - refugees from wars, exiles, outcasts with nowhere else to go. Before this effort, the territory was a no-man's land with no shared identity or unity. The act of naming gave these disparate people a sense of belonging and a collective identity for the first time.
- **Pre-Spera name — "The Cinderlands"**: Before unification, the region had no name of its own (its scattered inhabitants had only local settlement names, if any). The dismissive collective name used by *outsiders* — the surrounding nations who would never have set foot in it — was **the Cinderlands**. The name had a literal origin: in the years before the Death of Magic, the Original's feeding saturated a wide zone around its lair with such dense corruption that all life died (see Corrupted Magic → The Dead-Zone Exception). When the seal was cast, that whole zone froze in place: barren ground, dried-out tree skeletons, no animals, no growth — studded with the violet crystal formations into which the surrounding corruption had crystallized. To anyone who saw it (or heard the accounts that spread across the post-DoM continent), it looked exactly like burnt-out land — a country of cinder. Nature has slowly returned over the 2,300 years since, and much of the wider region is ordinary land again, but the name stuck and is still occasionally heard today, often pejoratively, when older or more traditional speakers refer to Spera or its people. The original reason the area was avoided (proximity to the Original's lair) had been forgotten by the time the name solidified into a generic insult, but the inherited sense of "cursed, empty, not for us" persists.
- Located in the north-center of the continent, north of the mountain range
- Formed by outcasts, exiles, the too-poor, the banned - people with nowhere else to go
- Settled the land nobody wanted: the most corrupted, most feared territory after the Death of Magic, full of violet crystal deposits and the lingering trauma of the organism
- The territory was genuinely abandoned and empty — no pact nation claimed it, and it was not the remnant of a fallen pact nation. It was reclaimed over thousands of years by people fleeing their own countries for any reason
- Contains the largest violet crystal deposit (the Original's prison) - though it remains undiscovered
- Industrial-scale violet crystal use caused the corruption
- Experiencing growing internal tensions and health issues, though most citizens are not yet alarmed
- A rare few have left the country over the years for various reasons
- Crime was historically very high - a frontier society built from desperate people
- Known cultural fact: accepts anyone within its borders. The *why* has been largely forgotten over the centuries; it was simply born of necessity
- The breakthrough in violet crystal processing (35 years ago) was their first real prosperity as a nation - a people who had always been the unwanted suddenly thriving
- Visual identity: steampunk-ish - violet crystal energy powering an industrial infrastructure unlike anything else in the world
- From their perspective, the political crisis feels like the old established nations attacking them again, as they always have

**Geography:**
- The capital and major cities are located in the far north-east of the nation
- This is where violet crystal use is densest, making the north-east the primary source of corruption
- Aldemar lies to the south, separated by mountains
- Vaeren lies to the east; their western population centers sit close to Spera's eastern cities, placing them nearest to the corruption's source

**Why Blue Crystals Aren't an Alternative:**
- The nation has very few blue crystal deposits
- This is because the Original's presence meant most magic in that region was already corrupted before the Death of Magic
- When magic crystallized, the ratio was skewed: mostly violet crystals, very little blue
- Before discovering how to process violet crystals, they were a poor and marginalized nation
- They survived on their few blue crystal quarries and conventional energy sources (coal, etc.)
- The breakthrough in violet crystal processing transformed them - the nation grew, and its people enjoyed prosperity for the first time
- Now they cannot easily go back

### Vaeren

- A pact-nation descendant — direct successor to the ancient Vaeren (the Shield nation), one of the original six pact nations. The modern nation has retained the ancient name across millennia, an unusual cultural continuity
- Pact role (ancestral): Underground creature containment, fortification, combat techniques
- Located in the northeast of the continent
- Sits atop the densest concentration of Hollow exits on the continent — the geographic basis of the original Shield role
- In tense conflict with Spera due to spreading corruption affecting their cities
- War has not yet been officially declared at the story's start

**Why the Shield Turned — The Invasion of Selvaren**:

When the Death of Magic broke the wards on dangerous underground exits, Vaeren faced an existential creature surge. Survival demanded heavy militarization, and the pact's role-distribution had matched Vaeren with the most warrior-minded otherworlders — populations chosen to fit the harshest assignment. It worked, for a while.

But the conditions never softened. Generation after generation of Vaereni inherited a life that was simply harder than what their cousins in the other pact nations lived. Children grew up watching parents die young to Hollows, and asking why their nation alone bore that cost while the others farmed, healed, sailed, and preserved in relative safety. Resentment grew slowly, across generations, the way grievances do when they are real and structural rather than acute.

Vaeren raised the issue at the Confluence repeatedly. **Selvaren** in particular heard them and tried to relay the concerns. By that era, however, the Confluence was already being looked down upon by the younger generations of the other nations — its sharing aspect was no longer universally appreciated, and the spirit of mutual obligation had thinned. Vaeren's complaints were not heard, or not taken seriously.

Then a particularly catastrophic Hollow break struck — worse than any in living memory. Something broke in Vaeren. Their next move was not another defensive push but the **invasion of Selvaren**. Selvaren occupied the fertile central-eastern lowlands directly southwest of Vaeren — temperate, well-watered, easy to cross — which made it both the natural first target for relieving the pressure and a strategic gateway deeper into the continent. The reasons were both straightforward and bitter: Selvaren held safer, gentler territory that would relieve the pressure, and Selvaren had been the one nation that *almost* listened — which made the deafness of the others sharper by contrast. Vaeren took the land. Selvaren as a polity ended.

This is what triggered the formation of Aldemar (see Aldemar above) and finished off the Confluence. The institution had already been weakening for generations — younger generations in the other nations had grown indifferent or hostile to the sharing principle, attendance had thinned, and commitment had eroded — but the invasion was the final shock that ended it outright. The pact era closed there.

**Modern Era**: The militarism remains as cultural inheritance but is no longer expansionist. The lived grievance of those generations is not the daily experience of modern Vaereni — they are a settled medieval nation now, with a hard reputation and a long memory. The current conflict with Spera draws on the old "fight evil from below the earth" identity rather than any new expansionist hunger.

**Geography:**
- Population centers are located in the west of the nation (their western border)
- This places them closer to the corruption spreading from Spera's eastern cities

**Religious Beliefs — "Tears of the Ancient Devil"**:
- Vaeren has a religious tradition tied to their oral heritage
- Violet crystals are called "Tears of the Ancient Devil" — tears shed by an evil being as it was defeated
- According to their belief, the Ancient Devil was defeated by mages of old
- Anything related to the devil is considered taboo
- This belief is mainstream in their religion, not a fringe view
- They have held this belief for thousands of years
- The religion fits the ancient Shield role: Vaeren has always defined itself by fighting evil from below the earth — first the Hollows, now the corruption rising from violet crystal processing

**Truth Preserved Through Distortion**:
- The "Ancient Devil" corresponds to the Original organism
- The "Tears" are crystallized corrupted magic
- "Defeated by mages" refers to the ancients sealing magic away
- The myth is wrong in details but correct in essence: violet crystals ARE dangerous, they ARE connected to something evil, and messing with them IS a bad idea

**Historical Dismissal**:
- Spera never accepted these beliefs
- They lived on their land for thousands of years without consequence from the unprocessed crystals
- Violet crystals were even used as jewelry in Spera — unprocessed crystals are harmless
- The religious warnings were dismissed as superstition
- Now that effects are appearing, Vaeren feels vindicated

## Archive Fragments

In-world textual fragments from archives, records, journals, inscriptions, and other written sources the player may encounter. These are canonical pieces of the world's written history and should be treated as diegetic content, not external references (those belong in `Documentation/Quotes.md`).

**Two categories** based on when the fragment was written:

- **Before the Death of Magic** — Written during the ancient civilization. Dates are precise and reliable because the ancients kept rigorous records and used a stable calendar system. Language is Ancient (unreadable to most modern people; the player can read it due to the summoning).
- **After the Death of Magic** — Written during or after the dark age. Dates are often approximate, contested, or unknown. The calendar system broke down; record-keeping collapsed; languages fragmented. Attribution and dating may rely on guesswork, context, or physical analysis of the materials.

### Entry Format

Each fragment should include:

- **Title / Identifier**: Short descriptive name
- **Date**: Use the calendar in effect at the time of writing (see Calendar System section). Ancient Calendar for pre-DoM fragments, Modern Calendar for post-DoM fragments (including dark-age fragments — dates from that period are often approximate or contested). Format: "Year X of the [Calendar Name], Month Day".
- **Origin**: Where found, who wrote it if known
- **Language**: Ancient, early post-DoM dialect, modern, etc.
- **Text**: The actual content of the fragment
- **Context / Notes**: What it reveals, hints at, or connects to. Any metadata relevant for placement in game.
