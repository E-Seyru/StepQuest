## Core Themes

### Narrative Tone
- **Slice of life with depth**: Balances everyday moments with serious, impactful events
- **Real stakes**: Characters can and will die. Betrayals happen. There will also be great times for the characters.
- **Found family**: The player builds bonds that may become more important than returning home

### What Drives Human Conflict
- The corruption does NOT affect human minds
- Fear, hunger, and desperation drive people to conflict
- Human nature under pressure - not magical mind control

### The Player's Motivation
- Primary: Wants to return to their own world, their home
- Secondary: As they bond with characters, that desire may fade or decrease
- Tertiary: Gets invested in the world's problems and people

## Magic System

### The Nature of Magic

**Origin**: Magic is a natural energy produced by the planet itself. It regenerates over time - even after being completely drained, the planet will slowly produce more over centuries and millennia.

**Ambient Magic**: Magic exists throughout the environment - atmosphere, ground, water. It permeates everything.

### How Magic Interacts with Life

**Passive Absorption**: All living beings naturally absorb ambient magic without conscious effort - humans, animals, plants, all life forms.

**Enhancement, Not Sustenance**: Magic does NOT sustain life. Creatures can survive without magic entirely. However, magic ENHANCES quality of life:
- Increased vitality and energy
- Sharper mental clarity
- Better physical health
- Improved crop yields
- General sense of well-being

**Symbiosis**: The relationship between the planet and its inhabitants is symbiotic. The planet produces magic; life benefits from it.

### Magic Users

- Magic exists and is accepted in society
- Not everyone can actively perform magic - this ability is rare
- The reasons why some can use magic and others cannot are not fully understood
- Magic is more common now than centuries ago, as ambient levels have been rising

### Otherworlders and Magic

**Natural Affinity**: People from non-magical worlds have a natural affinity for magic. Having never been exposed to it, they never built up tolerance. This makes perceiving and learning to manipulate magic easier for them. However, this affinity only affects active magic use — otherworlders absorb ambient magic passively at the same rate as anyone else.

**Not an Intended Gift**: This affinity is a natural consequence of their origin, not something the ancients deliberately gave them. While the ancients noted otherworlders' natural talent during the brief summoning period before the seal, this affinity became irrelevant once the seal took effect - there was no magic left to sense or use. Only now, with magic returning, would this trait actually matter.

**Not Heritable**: This affinity applies only to first-generation otherworlders - those actually summoned from non-magical worlds. Their children, born in a world with magic, develop normal tolerance like anyone else. Ancient records note that summoned people showed natural talent with magic, but their descendants displayed no such gift.

### Corrupted Magic

**Effects on Living Beings** (who passively absorb it):
- Makes humans sick (physical illness)
- Makes animals sick, which causes them to become violent and aggressive (pain response, not mind control)
- Causes crops to fail

**Visibility**: Corrupted magic affects ambient mana, not the physical landscape. The terrain itself is not visibly damaged or discolored. The effects are biological - sickness in living beings, crop failure - not environmental destruction. There is no visible atmospheric pollution or magical fog.

**Important**: Corrupted magic does NOT affect minds directly - not humans, not animals. It does not control thoughts or alter personalities. Animals become aggressive because they are in pain and discomfort from the physical sickness. Psychological effects in humans (paranoia, violence, cruelty) come from human reactions to the physical crisis - sickness, hunger, fear.

### Corrupted Magic Accumulation

**Persistence**: Corrupted magic accumulates indefinitely in the atmosphere. The planet only produces pure magic — it cannot cleanse or neutralize corruption. Pure and corrupted magic mix together in the ambient environment.

**Dilution**: Slow dilution over centuries is possible as the planet continues producing pure magic, but this is uncertain and not understood by people in the current era.

**Current Levels**: The corruption currently in the atmosphere is a tiny fraction of what existed during ancient times. Most violet crystal remains unprocessed — the majority of crystallized corrupted magic is still locked in the ground.

### Mage Sensitivity to Corruption

**Detection**: Mages can detect corrupted magic, but only when present in sufficient concentration. At low levels, it is imperceptible.

**Vulnerability**: Mages are more vulnerable to corruption than ordinary people. When practicing magic, mages must actively absorb surrounding ambient magic to use their abilities and replenish their reserves. This is far more magic-intensive than the passive absorption all beings experience. As a result, mages accumulate corrupted magic in their bodies faster than non-mages.

**Local Mages**: Mages who have lived their entire lives in the violet nation do not notice the gradual change in quality of ambient magic — they grew up with it slowly worsening and have no baseline for comparison.

**Foreign Mages**: Mages visiting from other nations can sense that something is "wrong" with the ambient magic — a faint trace of something off. However, they will not suffer health consequences without prolonged exposure.

**Timeline of Effects**:
- **Years 1-15**: Low corruption levels, slow accumulation. Toward the end of this period, the most active mages began experiencing slight effects (headaches, tiredness) — easily attributed to other causes. Most mages and the general population noticed nothing.
- **Years 15-25 (present)**: Corruption concentration significantly higher. Most active mages feel real hindrance in their practice. Some mages who practiced intensively throughout the past 25 years now have mid-to-severe health issues (mostly mid, severe cases are rare). Regular mages are only beginning to experience what the most active mages felt 10-15 years ago. Some individuals in the general population are starting to show effects.

**Acceleration**: Corruption accumulation is not linear — it is accelerating. Years 1-15 saw much lower corruption than years 15-25. Now that ambient magic is more concentrated with corruption, effects will manifest faster. The rate at which people become affected is increasing.

### Corruption Magic (Practiced)

- The organism was not present long enough for anyone to learn how to use corrupted magic in any significant way
- People knew it was possible through crude experiments, but that was the extent of it
- Even these crude experiments were enough to cause physical harm to the mages who attempted them
- This never developed into a real discipline or practice

## The Alien Organism

### Origin

- Arrived on the planet via meteorite during the height of the ancient civilization
- The meteorite impact created a deep crater; the Original was at the bottom
- Over millennia, the crater filled in naturally with sediment and rock
- An alien lifeform - not of this world
- Evolved to survive space travel: vacuum, extreme temperatures, absence of magic

### Nature

- Semi-conscious, similar to a fungus or plant
- Not intelligent - does not think, plan, or scheme
- Simply exists, feeds, grows, and reproduces
- The Original is physically indestructible by any means the ancients possessed

### Feeding and Corruption

- Feeds on pure magic only (cannot metabolize corrupted magic — it is its own waste product)
- When it consumes pure magic, it excretes corrupted magic as a byproduct
- This is its biological process - not intentional malice
- As it feeds, it saturates surroundings with corrupted magic

### Reproduction

- Reproduces via spores
- Spores grow into new organisms, which feed and release more spores
- Exponential growth pattern

### The Spores vs The Original

**The Spores**:
- NOT indestructible
- Could be killed, but required significant effort and specialized knowledge
- Not everyone could do it
- The problem was volume: too many, spreading too fast, everywhere
- Realistically impossible to eradicate them all
- Spores could be physically touched - they corrupted the magic they fed on, not physical matter directly
- However, proximity to spores caused nausea and illness due to the ambient corrupted magic they excreted

**The Original**:
- Physically indestructible
- Could survive without magic (evolved for space travel)
- When magic vanished, it entered hibernation rather than dying
- The only survivor of its kind on this planet
- Currently dormant

### Current Location

- The Original is entombed at the center of the largest violet crystal formation on the planet
- When the seal activated, the massive concentration of corrupted magic around it crystallized instantly
- It is trapped in crystallized magic - a prison created inadvertently by the seal
- This location is in the neighboring nation that began mining violet crystals
- The formation is currently underground, hidden beneath the filled-in crater
- It has NOT been discovered yet - the violet nation has been mining other, smaller deposits
- **Why It Remained Undiscovered**: In ancient times, cities were built as far from the Original's lair as possible. Life and development happened elsewhere. As centuries passed, the original reason was forgotten, but the pattern persisted — the area was simply ignored because it held no interest. Cities had developed elsewhere, and this location was just a place filled with nature, not interesting enough to invest in or build upon.
- The largest deposit will be discovered during the story, when mining operations nearby accidentally breach into the formation via tunnel

### Awakening Conditions

- Currently in deep hibernation
- The Original requires access to pure magic to awaken
- Its prison of crystallized corrupted magic completely cuts it off from the pure ambient magic in the atmosphere
- As long as the prison remains intact, it cannot access pure magic and cannot awaken
- The danger: once the formation is discovered and mining begins, operations will physically dismantle the prison from the outside
- If miners breach through to the Original, it will be exposed to ambient pure magic and awaken
- Once awakened, it will produce new spores, beginning a new cycle of exponential corruption

**Timeline**: The discovery of the largest deposit and subsequent mining will occur during the story. The player does not know about the Original or the threat at the start - they will uncover this truth gradually.

## Ancient History

### The Height of Civilization

- Far more advanced than the current era
- Magic was deeply integrated into society
- Technology and capabilities that seem miraculous to modern people
- Quality of life enhanced by abundant magic

### The Arrival and Infestation

- Meteorite brought the Original organism
- It fed, grew, released spores
- Spores spread, each one feeding and releasing more spores
- Corruption spread across large portions of the planet
- By the time the disastrous impact of the organism was discovered, it was already too late - the spores had already spread globally
- Local containment was no longer possible

### Attempts to Stop It

- The ancients tried to kill the spores
- It was possible but required effort and knowledge
- They could not keep up with the spread rate
- They attempted to destroy the Original - nothing worked (indestructible)

**The Cost to Mages:**
- Mages who attempted to destroy the Original or its spores paid a terrible price
- Casting spells in corruption-saturated areas required absorbing that corrupted magic directly into their bodies
- The first mages to confront the Original directly were unaware of this danger — they survived long enough to report back, but died in the days or weeks that followed
- Fighting the infestation was effectively suicide — the people most capable of stopping it were the most vulnerable to it

### The Decision to Seal

**Testing**: The ancients tested on captured spores and confirmed they died without access to magic. This was an informed decision.

**The Delay**: People could not resolve themselves to something so drastic. They spent time looking for a better resolution. During this delay, the civilization continued to decline as corruption spread further.

**Limited Time**: The Original and its spores produced corrupted magic far faster than what occurs with modern violet crystal processing. There was not much time to prepare for the aftermath.

**The Choice**: They knew sealing all magic would cause catastrophe - collapse of their civilization, mass death, a dark age. But there was no other option. The more time passed, the more magic became corrupted and the more civilization suffered. If left to spread further, no one would have survived.

**Multi-Nation Agreement**: The decision was made in agreement with most nations of that era — 6 nations signed the post-Death of Magic pact. Possibly not all nations agreed. The pact nations divided specialized roles among themselves for the rebuilding of civilization that would follow (see Pact Nation Roles below).

**The Fate of the Pact Nations**: Most of the original pact nations no longer exist as such. Over the millennia following the Death of Magic, wars, plagues, famine, and the general brutality of the dark age dissolved or transformed most of them. The pact nations were not naive about war - warfare existed in the ancient era - but they did not anticipate that the catastrophe itself would generate the conditions for it. The trauma, hunger, and desperation of the dark age produced conflicts of a scale and ferocity they had no framework to prepare for. Today only a handful of successor states remain, and smaller nations have formed since, some descended from pact fragments, others arising independently.

### Pact Nation Roles

**The Logic of Specialization**: The ancients knew that after the Death of Magic, communication and travel between nations would be extremely difficult — no teleportation, weakened animals, collapsed infrastructure. No single nation could master everything needed to rebuild civilization. Each nation was assigned a specialized role based on the geographic advantages of the territory they would inhabit. Each would develop deep expertise in one domain and share it with the others.

**The 10-Year Festival**: To share knowledge between nations, the pact established a gathering every 10 years — a massive festival where each nation presented the advancements they had made in their assigned domain. This was not just celebration; it was load-bearing infrastructure for civilization's survival. Techniques, discoveries, and innovations developed by one nation were disseminated to all. When the festivals eventually stopped — due to wars, collapsed nations, and broken trust — knowledge transfer stopped with them. Each surviving nation kept only what they had specialized in, losing access to the rest.

**The Six Roles**:

| Role | Specialization | Geographic Basis |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| **The Sowers** | Agriculture, food production, animal husbandry | Most fertile plains and valleys |
| **The Menders** | Medicine, healing, managing magic withdrawal | Region rich in medicinal flora and resources |
| **The Shapers** | Construction, tools, metallurgy, engineering | Mineral-rich, mountainous terrain |
| **The Shields** | Underground creature containment, fortification, combat techniques | Densest concentration of underground cave exits |
| **The Keepers** | Knowledge preservation, archiving history and ancient records | Custodians of the most important ancient archives |
| **The Mariners** | Shipbuilding, navigation, fishing, sea transport | Forested coastline with natural harbors |

**Why These Roles**: Each maps to a critical survival need that could be geographically concentrated. The Sowers needed the best farmland. The Shapers needed mineral deposits. The Shields needed to be where the underground exits were densest. The Mariners needed coastline and timber. The Menders needed access to medicinal resources. The Keepers were entrusted with the most important surviving archives and records.

**The Mariners' Critical Function**: With overland travel crippled (weakened animals, no magical transport), sea and river routes became the primary means of moving large quantities of goods between nations. The Mariners' ships carried the Shapers' metal, the Sowers' grain, and the delegates to each festival. They were arguably the connective tissue of the pact.

**Role Survival in the Modern Era**: Most pact roles have been forgotten or corrupted beyond recognition over the millennia. Some nations retain cultural echoes of their original purpose without understanding the origin. The mapping of ancient roles to modern nations is still being determined (see Nations section).

### The Teleportation Hub

**Original Purpose**: A central facility in the mountains, built for inter-nation teleportation. Located in a remote mountain pass, isolated from cities - this isolation meant all available ambient magic in the area could be dedicated to teleportation, which would not have been possible in populated areas where magic was used for many purposes. This made it the natural hub for all inter-nation travel.

**Conversion for the Pact**: When the decision to seal was made, most teleportation devices at the hub were converted into otherworlder summoning portals, to bring as many otherworlders as possible before the seal. Some teleportation devices were kept operational for the evacuation - moving populations to their assigned pact nations.

**Why Centralization Was Essential**:
- **Skill distribution**: All otherworlders arrived at the same place, allowing the ancients to assess their skills and compose balanced groups
- Although same-planet groups were kept together, distribution to nations was done with utility in mind
- Each group sent to a nation was carefully composed to ensure they had enough of each type of knowledge (farmers, healers, builders, etc.)
- If each nation had summoned separately, they would have received random skill distributions — potentially disastrous for survival

**Creating a Safe Zone**:
- The site required a coordinated effort by all nations to make it safe for mages to work
- All spores in the area were successfully eradicated
- The zone was closely monitored throughout the summoning operation
- This allowed mages to build and operate the devices without the lethal corruption exposure they would face elsewhere

**Why Proximity Still Mattered**:
- All living things experienced drawbacks from the lack of magic — not just humans
- Animals bred faster than humans, so new generations without withdrawal effects emerged more quickly
- However, during the immediate aftermath, horses and other beasts of burden were affected and could not carry loads as they would have in the past
- This made long-distance travel difficult, which is why even a central location was better than building devices at the edges of the known world

**The Casting of the Seal**: After populations were evacuated to their designated nations, the Death of Magic spell was cast at the hub. Approximately 100 of the most competent mages remained to perform the spell. A few hundred otherworlders stayed with them - not to help cast, but to help the mages survive the immediate aftermath, as the mages would be struck by magic withdrawal the moment the spell took effect. After the spell was cast, the otherworlders helped the weakened mages descend by river through the mountains to the northern island.

**The Northern Island Group**: The mages and otherworlders who retreated to the northern island were unique — they were the only group that had not been teleported to their designated nation. They traveled on foot and by river, which meant they were the only people who knew the physical route to the hub and the locations of the summoning devices. This group's otherworlders were the ones who, 5-10 years later, found and destroyed the summoning devices (see The Summoning Devices below). Their knowledge of the hub's location was exclusive, and their descendants will be important later in the story.

**Current State**: The hub is now ancient ruins at the center of the mountain range, overgrown and partially collapsed after millennia. Remains of the teleportation devices can still be found — broken in many parts, covered by vegetation. The site was completely abandoned after the devices were destroyed. Over the centuries, some people came and dismantled pieces for various reasons. Modern people have a vague idea what it was — possibly remembered as "devices used to summon an army against the Ancient Devil" or similar distorted accounts. It is not recognized for what it truly was. This site is distinct from the player's summoning device, which was a prototype built separately.

### The Death of Magic

- Through means now lost, they crystallized ALL magic on the planet
- Pure magic became blue crystals
- Corrupted magic became violet crystals
- All ambient magic vanished
- The spores died (confirmed by prior testing)
- The Original did not die - it entered hibernation

### The Aftermath

- Everything built on magic stopped working
- Infrastructure collapsed
- Famine, disease, social collapse
- Mass death
- Humanity survived but was thrown back to primitive conditions
- Most knowledge was lost
- Thousands of years of slow rebuilding to reach current medieval level

### Magic Withdrawal

The population of the ancient era had absorbed magic their entire lives. When magic vanished, their bodies suffered withdrawal.

**Symptoms:**
- Physical weakness and fatigue
- Mental fog, difficulty concentrating
- Depression and emotional instability
- Chronic pain or discomfort
- More prone to sickness, with more severe symptoms
- Shortened lifespan in severe cases

**Severity**: Most people were not completely debilitated, but they were diminished - easily tired, more vulnerable to illness, bodies struggling to function without magic.

**Duration**: A single generation. Those who experienced the Death of Magic directly took a few years to a decade for their bodies to accommodate and learn to live without magic. Children born after the seal did not experience withdrawal.

**Why Otherworlders Were Essential**: Summoned people from non-magical worlds had never absorbed magic. They did not experience withdrawal. While the native population struggled with diminished capacity, otherworlders were fully functional - healthy workers who could help maintain society during the crisis.

### Lost Knowledge and Archives

**What Remains**: Ancient ruins stand as testament to the old civilization's might. Archives and records exist in various locations.

**Non-Magical Writings**: Some non-magical writings were left in people's hands after the Death of Magic, and some were written afterward. However, most have been lost to time, and those that remain cannot be read due to the language barrier.

**Why It's Inaccessible**: Ancient knowledge is double-locked:
1. **Language barrier**: The Ancient language has been lost to time. No one can read the texts.
2. **Magic barrier**: Archives require active magic input to open — the seals are mechanisms that only respond to deliberate magical manipulation, not passive barriers.

**Why the Language Was Lost**: The otherworlders summoned during the Death of Magic were taught the Ancient language through the summoning process, but they still spoke their own native languages. Otherworlders came from different non-magical worlds, though not a huge number of worlds — some groups were summoned from the same world and stayed together.

These people were unwillingly summoned to another world. They could not return home — without magic, the summoning devices were useless. Preserving their language and culture was the least they could do. Since going back was impossible, they opted for the second-best thing: making this place feel like home.

Since many natives were weakened by magic withdrawal, otherworlders became some of the most important and influential people of their time. Over generations, the true Ancient language was modified beyond recognition and eventually lost — competing with multiple otherworlder languages, and lacking practical use (the archives were inaccessible anyway).

**The Dark Centuries**: In the centuries immediately following the Death of Magic, archives were completely inaccessible — there was literally no magic to work with. Even if someone could somehow read the language, no one could use magic to open the sealed archives. This period lasted centuries, possibly a millennium.

**Modern Attempts**: As magic gradually returned, some mages managed to open archives over the centuries. However, many archives have been damaged or lost to time — millennia of neglect, wars, natural decay, and deliberate destruction during the dark age took their toll. Scholars have been studying the Ancient language, and some words or context can now be understood at a surface level. However, this understanding remains too fragmentary to extract meaningful knowledge from complex texts.

**Current Understanding**: People know a great civilization existed and fell. They do not know how or why. Various theories exist, but nothing is certain. The truth — scattered across surviving archives that can now be physically opened but not read — awaits someone who can understand the Ancient language.

### Knowledge Degradation Over Time

**Ancient Era**: People understood the distinction between pure and corrupted magic. They knew blue crystals were crystallized pure magic and violet crystals were crystallized corrupted magic.

**Post-Death of Magic**: With no ambient magic, no one could use either type of crystal. The practical distinction became irrelevant — both were inert, useless stones.

**Over Millennia**: Without practical relevance, the "why" behind the distinction was forgotten. Only vague warnings survived. Written records were mostly lost — they were in the Ancient language, and no one saw practical importance in preserving warnings about stones they couldn't use anyway. The first generations after the Death of Magic may have cared, but subsequent generations did not, and the knowledge faded.

**Modern Understanding**: Before the processing breakthrough, people simply thought violet crystals were a variant of blue crystals that they hadn't figured out how to use yet. The two look similar enough that this assumption was reasonable.

### Surviving Oral Tradition

Despite the loss of written knowledge, some memories survived through oral tradition - though warped by thousands of years of retelling.

**What Death of Magic Era Survivors Tried to Preserve:**
- A terrible monster destroyed the old world
- The monster's lair became covered in violet crystal after the catastrophe
- Violet crystals appeared in places where corruption was worst
- Violet was linked to corruption and to the monster

**What Actually Survived (Modern Era):**
- The specific details above degraded into vague, childlike tales over millennia
- No actionable warnings remain - just fuzzy folklore
- The color violet is associated with bad luck, ill omens, or misfortune
- Stories to frighten children, not serious historical knowledge
- No one knows WHERE the monster's lair was, or what specifically to avoid
- Most educated people consider these stories primitive myths with no factual basis

**How Warnings Were Dismissed:**
- When early violet crystal processing experiments showed no immediate harm, scholars dismissed the old warnings as baseless superstition
- The gradual accumulation of harm let people rationalize: "If it were truly dangerous, we'd have seen it immediately"
- By the time the warnings proved true, the neighboring nation was already dependent on violet crystal energy

### The Underground Threat

**Nature**: Beneath the continent lies an extensive network of deep cave systems harboring an entire subterranean ecosystem. Magic density increases with depth — the closer to the planet's core, the denser the ambient magic. As a result, creatures dwelling deeper underground are more magical and more dangerous, while those near the surface are largely non-magical and less threatening. The underground is a gradient from mundane to extraordinarily dangerous.

**Ancient Management**: In ancient times, mages sealed all dangerous exits from the underground — the deep access points where powerful, magical creatures could surface. However, some entrances remained open and regulated, used for hunting and gathering rare materials from the creatures within. Venturing into the upper levels of the caves was a known, managed activity — dangerous but profitable.

**Post-Death of Magic**: When magic vanished, the magical wards on sealed exits broke. The deeper, magical creatures were weakened or killed by the loss of magic — those most dependent on ambient magic could not survive without it. However, the non-magical and less magic-dependent creatures of the upper and mid levels were unaffected. With the wards gone, these creatures began surfacing freely through exits across the continent.

**Why They Cannot Be Eradicated**: The cave systems extend deeper than anyone can explore. The subterranean ecosystem is vast and self-sustaining. Humans can only manage the surface exits — they cannot invade the underground world and eliminate the source population. The creatures surface for food, territory, and breeding cycles. It is persistent animal behavior — endless, like tides.

**Distribution**: Every nation has some cave exits to manage, but one region sits atop the densest concentration — essentially the roof of the largest underground network. This became the basis for the Shield nation's pact role (see Pact Nation Roles).

**Modern Era**: As magic has slowly returned (~50% of ancient levels), the underground ecosystem is shifting again. Deeper creatures that were dormant or weakened are regaining strength. Some mages have helped re-seal certain exits, but the threat remains very much present. The underground gradient of magical density means that as ambient magic continues to rise, increasingly dangerous creatures may begin surfacing again.

## The Dark Age

The period immediately following the Death of Magic — characterized by collapse, survival, and the eventual rebuilding of civilization.

### Otherworlder-Native Relations

**The Guilt of the Ancients**: The ancient mages and elders were acutely aware of what they had inflicted on the summoned people. They had forcibly brought strangers to a dying world. This guilt shaped every interaction.

**How Natives Treated Otherworlders**:
- They treated otherworlders with kindness
- They explained everything about what had happened and why
- They apologized throughout — both to those summoned right before the Death of Magic and to those summoned after, when the natives had become powerless and frail
- The ancient mages taught everything they could to the otherworlders

**How Otherworlders Responded**:
- Most felt resentment — this was natural and expected
- With time and the kindness shown by the elders, they managed to get by
- Some even came to appreciate their new lives
- Those who still felt hatred generally did not show it or act on it — they understood their predicament
- Rare cases of outward hostility were dealt with by their peers (other otherworlders)

**Division of Roles**:
- Otherworlders were the "arms" of this new era — the healthy workforce that could function without magic
- Ancient mages were the "brains" — they possessed the knowledge and taught it to the otherworlders

### Organization of Otherworlders

**Keeping Origin Groups Together**: The ancient mages, out of guilt and humanity, made it possible for people from the same origin world to remain together. This was a deliberate kindness.

**Assignment to Nations**:
- Groups of otherworlders (thousands to tens of thousands) were assigned to different nations
- Each group was accompanied by mages from that nation
- The nations of that era are not the same as current nations

**Pre-Death Planning**:
- Before the Death of Magic, heads of nations reunited to plan for the aftermath
- Each nation was assigned a specific role in the rebuilding
- The Third Nation (the neighboring nation in our story) was assigned the role of "Those Who Remember" — their duty was to preserve knowledge and prevent future catastrophes of this scale

### The Collapse

Despite the planning and cooperation, the situation was catastrophic.

**The Scale of Death**:
- Millions died
- When otherworlders reached their respective nations, they found panic, desperation, sickness, and famine
- There was not enough food for everyone
- Food production would take time to ramp up

**Wars for Resources**:
- Some nations united to wage war against others in desperation for resources
- Some of these wars were led by otherworlders
- Trust between nations quickly crumbled

**What Was Lost**:
- Some nations fell entirely
- Some roles assigned to nations before the Death of Magic were completely lost to history as those nations collapsed
- Ancient nations do not map to current nations — the political landscape was reshaped by this era

**Governments**:
- Governments survived the Death of Magic initially
- However, all nations' governments underwent significant changes following the integration of otherworlders and the pressures of survival

## The Crystals

### Using Crystals

- Using crystals to power anything requires ambient magic to exist
- Crystals cannot be used in isolation - there must be magic in the environment to interface with them
- In the centuries immediately following the Death of Magic, crystals were useless - there was no ambient magic to work with
- This is why the ancients could not use crystallized magic to sustain their economy after the seal

### Corrupted Magic and Devices

- Magical devices (artifacts, summoning devices, etc.) can function with corrupted magic
- Corrupted magic harms living beings and the environment, not mechanical devices
- A device powered by corrupted magic works normally - the harm is to the surroundings, not the device itself

### Blue Crystals (Pure Magic)

- Crystallized pure magic from the seal event
- Safe energy source
- Found in various locations

### Violet Crystals (Corrupted Magic)

- Crystallized corrupted magic from the seal event
- 2 to 5 times more potent than blue crystals as energy (potency varies)
- Concentrated in areas where the infestation was worst during ancient times
- The largest deposit is the prison of the Original organism
- Using them releases corrupted magic back into the environment

## The Modern Era

### Current State

**Technology Level**: High Medieval
- Castles, knights, established kingdoms
- Guilds and structured trades
- Steel weapons and armor

**Magic's Return**: The planet has been producing magic again over millennia. Ambient levels are slowly rising. Magic users have reappeared, though still rare. Current ambient magic levels are roughly 50% of what existed during ancient times — significant enough for mages to practice and crystals to function, but far below the saturation of the old era.

**Ancient Artifacts**:
- Ancient artifacts originally did not work with crystals at all - they drew power from ambient magic
- Mages can also input their own magic into artifacts directly to boost their power
- Ancient mages had more mana available and could inject more into artifacts
- Over centuries, people experimented with old, mysterious artifacts
- They tried injecting magic directly and got partial results (because ambient magic today is still below ancient levels, and modern mages have less mana than ancient ones)
- This is how people knew the artifacts worked with magic, but a single modern mage rarely has enough magic to power them alone
- Researchers eventually found ways to use crystals to power some artifacts

### Discovery of Violet Crystals

**What Was Known:**
- The violet crystal formations have been documented and known for centuries
- They were recognized as different from blue crystals, but nobody could use them

**The Breakthrough:**
- The recent "discovery" was a breakthrough in PROCESSING - a method to finally convert violet crystals into usable energy
- This breakthrough occurred 25 years ago
- As with all periods of time, researchers had been trying to solve energy issues and improve quality of life - such experiments eventually yielded success
- This breakthrough unlocked access to a power source 2-5x more potent than blue crystals

**Mining Timeline:**
- Violet crystals are abundant throughout the nation — there was never a need to actively survey for more deposits
- The first years after the breakthrough were spent developing infrastructure: manufacturing mines, equipment, and processing facilities
- Mining started slowly and became more efficient over time
- 25 years of gradual expansion has brought operations close to discovering the largest deposit (the Original's prison)

**Prototype Exports:**
- The processing technique remains a closely guarded secret of the Violet Nation
- However, some prototype processing devices have been sold to wealthy foreign nobles
- These devices saw a warm welcome from those rich enough to afford them
- Due to low local use over a short time, no visible health incidents have occurred outside the Violet Nation from these exports

**What the Energy Is Used For:**
- This is NOT industrial in the sense of steam engines or heavy machinery
- Violet crystal energy powers utilities: light, heat, and some ancient magical devices
- The neighboring nation became dependent on this energy for basic survival needs - heating homes, lighting cities

**Why They Can't Stop:**
- The nation's population grew during the 25 years of violet crystal usage
- Stopping extraction would cause catastrophic consequences without external help — famine in regions dependent on violet-powered agriculture, deaths from cold in areas relying on violet crystal heating
- The population has grown beyond what conventional energy sources can sustain
- This is an energy crisis, not villainy - an Oppenheimer-style tragedy
- They unlocked a power source they cannot easily live without, but which is actively poisoning the world

### The Crisis

**What Happened**:
- Mining extracts violet crystals (the extraction itself is not the primary problem)
- Converting/processing violet crystals into usable energy is what releases corrupted magic
- This conversion happens locally - in homes, cities, wherever the crystals are used for heat, light, or powering devices
- People fell ill, animals became aggressive, crops failed
- Effects spread from population centers and usage points throughout the nation, not from the mines

**Why It Wasn't Stopped**:
- Early researchers did not know violet crystals were dangerous - small-scale processing released too little corrupted magic to cause noticeable harm
- The danger emerged gradually over years of widespread usage as corrupted magic accumulated in the atmosphere
- Health effects began appearing perhaps 10-15 years ago, with gradual increase over time
- By the time patterns emerged and people began to have doubts, the nation was already dependent on violet crystal energy
- This was not immediate knowledge followed by greed - it was slow realization followed by economic and infrastructural inertia
- The corruption is insidious: ordinary citizens using violet energy to heat their homes or light their streets all contribute to the problem

**Current State of Spera**:
- Health issues appearing in the population, especially among active mages
- Growing internal debate about whether violet crystals are the cause
- Researchers being called in to investigate
- Most citizens are not yet alarmed enough to take drastic action
- A rare few have left the country

**Cross-Border Spread**:
- Corrupted magic released into the atmosphere gradually spreads beyond the violet nation's borders
- Carried by wind and weather patterns over the years
- After 25 years of accumulation, neighboring nations are beginning to experience effects

**The Conflict**:
- A third nation is in tense conflict with Spera because they believe the spreading corruption is affecting their cities
- War has not yet been officially declared at the story's start - the situation is at the tension/negotiation phase
- War may be officially declared during the game
- The third nation's religious beliefs (violet crystals as "Tears of the Ancient Devil") add a moral/religious dimension to their objections
- Spera dismisses these concerns as superstition and unproven accusations
- Extraction continues

**Why They Won't Stop**:
- Stopping extraction means losing the conflict, losing the means to sustain the country, losing power
- Those responsible argue that the crystals are NOT causing the problems
- They deny the connection between extraction and the degradation of health, crops, and land
- Official position: the corruption has other causes; the crystals are safe and essential

### The Player's Nation

- Currently stable
- Not yet directly affected by corruption - the violet crystal processing sites are in the neighboring nation
- Aware of tensions between Spera and the third nation, but not directly involved

## The Player Character

### Origin

- Isekai - transported from another world (modern Earth or similar)
- No special powers or chosen one status
- An ordinary person

### Why They Were Summoned

**The Summoning Devices:**
- Built by the ancients before the Death of Magic
- Original purpose: Summon people from non-magical worlds to help survivors rebuild after magic vanished
- The devices detect non-magical worlds specifically - they target realities where magic does not exist
- The devices had no means of selecting for specific skills or knowledge - they only targeted worlds without magic
- The most important thing was that summoned people could live in a world without magic - since many people were summoned, useful knowledge came statistically with the masses
- Automatic function: accumulate magic energy → summon → repeat
- In ancient times, mages fed the devices magic to maximize the summoning rate

**How It Worked in Ancient Times:**
- Devices activated right before/during the Death of Magic
- Summoned people were greeted by guides, explained the situation, given purpose
- These summoned people helped humanity survive the dark age
- They taught survivors how to live without the crutch of magic

**What Happened to the Player:**
- Most summoning devices were deliberately destroyed by otherworlders themselves, 5-10 years after the Death of Magic
- The ancient mages were honest about the situation: magic, in a quantity sufficient to reactivate these devices, would take centuries to return
- Despite knowing this, otherworlders initially held hope that the devices could somehow be used to return home. Many tried for years to make them work — to find any magic
- Hope led to despair. Their people could not move on while clinging to impossible hope
- A small group of otherworlders (a minority, but determined) made the choice to find and destroy the devices, so their people could finally accept this world as home
- The majority probably wanted to keep the devices safe and secured, but this group acted anyway after seeing the depressed state of close relatives clinging to false hope
- These otherworlders were from the northern island group — the ones who had stayed with the mages to cast the seal. They were the only people who knew the physical locations of the devices, since every other group had been teleported away and never traveled the route on foot
- This device survived because it was a prototype — a proof of concept built during the initial research phase, before the hub was even operational. It was built wherever the research happened to succeed first, far from the hub. There was no reason or ability to build near the hub during the research stage
- It is one of the last remaining, possibly the last
- After the Death of Magic, it sat dormant (no magic to accumulate)
- As magic slowly returned to the world, the device accumulated energy - incredibly slowly
- After thousands of years, it finally had enough for one summon
- It activated and pulled the player through
- It's a dumb machine - no awareness that the world changed, no understanding that its purpose is obsolete

**Why the Device Wasn't Discovered:**
- Ancient ruins are everywhere in the world - remnants of the old civilization
- This device was treated as just one more old ruin among many
- Over the millennia, people likely tried injecting magic into it without success
- They concluded that whatever it was, it no longer worked - not understanding it needed far more energy than any modern mage could provide
- A summoning requires massive amounts of power; casual experimentation would yield nothing

**Why Only One Person:**
- Barely enough accumulated energy for a single summon
- Device is ancient, possibly damaged or degraded
- Was supposed to summon more people
- Was supposed to have guidance waiting

**Why Now - The Correlation:**
- The player's arrival during a time of crisis is not prophecy or destiny
- It is cause and effect: violet crystal processing releases corrupted magic into the atmosphere, rapidly raising ambient magic levels
- This accelerated rise in ambient magic finally gave the summoning device enough power to activate
- The same mining operations are physically dismantling the Original's prison
- The summoning and the potential awakening share the same underlying cause: the processing of violet crystals

**The Player's Situation:**
- Summoned for a purpose that no longer exists (helping a world without magic)
- Arrives in a world where magic has returned
- No guides, no welcome, no explanation - that infrastructure is long gone
- An anachronism, an accident, a glitch in ancient machinery
- Not chosen, not destined - just someone pulled through by a machine blindly following ancient programming
- Any importance they gain is earned through actions, not destiny

### Otherworlder Abilities

**Broad Language Comprehension**: The summoning process grants the ability to understand and speak all languages. This was an imperative — otherworlders were summoned from multiple different worlds and spoke entirely different languages from each other, not just from the natives. Without universal comprehension, otherworlders could not communicate with natives, with each other, or coordinate the survival effort. The summoning imbued this ability automatically.

**Ancient Language — The Unique Gift**: This broad comprehension includes the Ancient language, which is now extinct. Modern inhabitants cannot read it — the language was lost over millennia as otherworlder languages competed with and eventually replaced it. The player is one of the only people alive who can read Ancient texts, a direct and unique consequence of the summoning process.

**Magic Affinity**: Like all otherworlders, the player has natural sensitivity to magic due to coming from a non-magical world (see Magic System > Otherworlders and Magic).

**Why This Matters Now**: The player is potentially the first person in millennia who can both read Ancient texts AND access magically-sealed archives. They could unlock knowledge that has been inaccessible since the Death of Magic.

### Being Discovered

People will eventually deduce the player is an otherworlder from old tales. The signs:
- Strange behavior (doesn't know basic customs or common knowledge)
- Can read Ancient script that no one else understands
- Has magic affinity as an adult with zero training

Any one trait is odd. All three together points to one conclusion.

### The Way Home

**The Goal**: The player wants to return to their world, their home.

**The Portal**: The summoning device/portal that brought the player is located in the forest. After millennia, it is covered by roots, trees, and vegetation - almost not visible anymore. It does not currently function.

**The Path**: The exact method to return home is not yet known to the player. Possibilities may involve reverse-engineering the portal, discovering new magic, or finding Ancient knowledge about the summoning process.

### The Temptation

At some point in the story, an important figure from the violet crystal nation will approach the player. They will offer to help the player find a way home. This creates moral conflict - potentially accepting help from those causing the world's crisis in exchange for achieving a personal goal.

### Arrival Circumstances

- Appears in a forest, at night, during rain
- Completely disoriented and frightened
- Possibly brief glimpse of their normal life just before transportation (so the player understands what happened)

### Initial Situation

- Attacked by a low-level creature
- Clearly outmatched and terrified
- Rescued by guards who heard the commotion

### How They're Perceived

- Guards see their visible disorientation and fear
- The player's inability to explain themselves ("I have no idea what's going on")
- Their struggle against a low-level threat proves they're not dangerous
- The guards focus on the player's behavior and state, not their strange clothing
- Small town in peacetime — guards have no reason for paranoia
- Guards conclude: a lost, confused person in need of help — not a threat

### Status

- Brought to the village
- Placed in the care of a local caretaker — someone who helps people in need (lost travelers, the displaced, those who fall through the cracks)
- The caretaker serves as tutorial, helping the player learn how things work
- Player must earn their keep

### The Countdown

- The player has a visible countdown displayed in-game, starting at approximately 3 years (subject to tuning)
- At the start, the player does not know what the countdown represents or what happens when it reaches zero
- The countdown is tied to the Original's awakening — when it reaches zero, the Original breaks free, produces new spores, and the world ends. Modern mages are not capable enough to replicate the Death of Magic spell or neutralize the Original
- The player discovers what the countdown means gradually through the story
- The countdown is NOT fixed — it changes based on the player's choices and actions within the story. Player decisions can accelerate or delay the countdown
- This is the core tension mechanic: visible, urgent stakes whose meaning unfolds over time

### Visual Transition

- The player arrives in modern clothing from their world
- The caretaker provides the player with local clothing
- This serves as the player's first "equipment" upgrade
- Marks the visual transition into the world - the player now looks like they belong

### Spera (The Violet Nation)

- **Not a pact nation** - a completely separate origin
- **Name origin**: "Spera" means hope. Named only a few hundred years ago - the first nation name of its kind, not derived from ancient pact-era naming conventions. The name was chosen by the first great groups who attempted to unite the scattered people hiding within this land - refugees from wars, exiles, outcasts with nowhere else to go. Before this effort, the territory was a no-man's land with no shared identity or unity. The act of naming gave these disparate people a sense of belonging and a collective identity for the first time.
- Located in the north-center of the continent, north of the mountain range
- Formed by outcasts, exiles, the too-poor, the banned - people with nowhere else to go
- Settled the land nobody wanted: the most corrupted, most feared territory after the Death of Magic, full of violet crystal deposits and the lingering trauma of the organism
- The territory was genuinely abandoned and empty — no pact nation claimed it, and it was not the remnant of a fallen pact nation. It was reclaimed over thousands of years by people fleeing their own countries for any reason
- Contains the largest violet crystal deposit (the Original's prison) - though it remains undiscovered
- Industrial violet crystal processing caused the corruption
- Experiencing growing internal tensions and health issues, though most citizens are not yet alarmed
- A rare few have left the country over the years for various reasons
- Crime was historically very high - a frontier society built from desperate people
- Known cultural fact: accepts anyone within its borders. The *why* has been largely forgotten over the centuries; it was simply born of necessity
- The breakthrough in violet crystal processing (25 years ago) was their first real prosperity as a nation - a people who had always been the unwanted suddenly thriving
- Visual identity: steampunk-ish - violet crystal energy powering an industrial infrastructure unlike anything else in the world
- From their perspective, the political crisis feels like the old established nations attacking them again, as they always have

**Geography:**
- The capital and major cities are located in the far north-east of the nation
- This is where most violet crystal processing occurs, making the north-east the primary source of corruption
- The player's nation lies to the south, separated by mountains
- The Third Nation lies to the east; their western population centers sit close to Spera's eastern cities, placing them nearest to the corruption's source

**Why Blue Crystals Aren't an Alternative:**
- The nation has very few blue crystal deposits
- This is because the Original's presence meant most magic in that region was already corrupted before the Death of Magic
- When magic crystallized, the ratio was skewed: mostly violet crystals, very little blue
- Before discovering how to process violet crystals, they were a poor and marginalized nation
- They survived on their few blue crystal quarries and conventional energy sources (coal, etc.)
- The breakthrough in violet crystal processing transformed them - the nation grew, and its people enjoyed prosperity for the first time
- Now they cannot easily go back
